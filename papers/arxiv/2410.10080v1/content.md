::: ltx_abstract
###### Abstract {#abstract .ltx_title .ltx_title_abstract}

As the 50G optical access gradually matures, it is time to discuss Beyond 50G optical access. According to the evolution rules of optical access standards, Beyond 50G optical access data rate may achieve 200Gb/s. Direct detection faces great challenges for Beyond 50G optical access, which makes coherent detection a potential solution. Similar to 50G optical timing-division-multiple access (TDMA), burst-mode digital signal processing (BM-DSP) is also required for Beyond 50G coherent optical TDMA (CO-TDMA). This paper proposes coherent BM-DSP (Co-BM-DSP) based on approximately 10ns designed preambles to process the burst signal for 200G CO-TDMA, which can fast estimate the state of polarization, frequency offset, sampling phase offset, synchronization position, and equalizer coefficients. Meanwhile, for obtaining the equalizer coefficients based on the designed preamble, the channel estimation based on the minimum-mean-square-error criterion is theoretically proven to have a unique solution for ensuring reliability. In conclusion, the proposed Co-BM-DSP based on the designed preambles paves the way for the applications of Beyond 50G CO-TDMA.

## [I ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Introduction]{#S1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#i-introduction .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S1.p1 .ltx_para}
IN 2021, significant progress was made in the International Telecommunication Union Telecommunication (ITU-T) standardization sector to define a higher-speed (HS) optical access with a line rate of 50Gb/s \[[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib1){.ltx_ref}, [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib2){.ltx_ref}, [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}\]. Recently, 50G optical access has gradually matured with the efforts of vendors and operators. It is time to discuss and research Beyond 50G optical access. ITU-T establishes a group for discussing future optical-access-related technologies for Beyond 50G optical access\[[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib4){.ltx_ref}, [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib5){.ltx_ref}, [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib6){.ltx_ref}\]. There are two views on the line rate of Beyond 50G optical access: 100Gb/s or 200Gb/s. Based on the evolution rules of ITU-T standards, the data rate of optical access is 4 or 5 times higher than that of previous-generation optical access. Thus, Beyond 50G optical access data rate may achieve 200Gb/s \[[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref}, [8](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, [9](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\]. For the 200G optical access, direct detection (DD) faces tough challenges. Coherent detection becomes the most potential solution \[[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}, [11](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}, [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}\].
:::

Owing to statistical multiplexing, time division multiple access (TDMA) has been used to ensure capacity and quantity for the subscribers since the beginning of commercial applications for the optical access \[[13](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref}, [14](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib14){.ltx_ref}, [15](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}\]. In 50G DD optical TDMA, one burst-mode trans-impedance amplifier (BM-TIA) and burst-mode digital signal processing (BM-DSP) are required to receive and process the upstream burst signal, which has a short convergence time to improve spectral efficiency \[[16](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref}, [17](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib17){.ltx_ref}, [18](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref}\]. Similarly, coherent optical TDMA (CO-TDMA) shown in Fig. [[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ I Introduction ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref} needs four BM-TIAs and coherent BM-DSP (Co-BM-DSP) to process the burst polarization-division-multiplexing (PDM) in-phase and quadrature (IQ) signals such as PDM quadrature phase shift keying and 16-quadrature amplitude modulation (16QAM) signal \[[19](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}, [20](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, [21](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}\]. The difficulty of the Co-BM-DSP is that the state of polarization (SOP) and frequency offset cause estimation errors in the sampling phase offset (SPO), synchronization position, and tap coefficients of the equalizer. Therefore, except for the SPO, synchronization position, and tap coefficients, the Co-BM-DSP should quickly estimate the SOP and frequency offset \[[22](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}, [23](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}, [24](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}\].
:::

::: {#S1.p3 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we design 10ns preambles with 320 symbols for the Co-BM-DSP in 200G CO-TDMA based on a 32GBaud PDM-16QAM signal. The two frequency tones with 128 symbols on X polarization and Y polarization are used to estimate SOP, frequency offset, and SPO. The 3$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}64 constant amplitude zero auto-correlation (CAZAC) symbols are used to obtain the synchronization position and equalizer coefficients. This paper is an extended version of our previous work \[[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref}\] published on ECOC 2024, which provides more details about principle of Co-BM-DSP, discussion of the experimental results, and reliability analysis for channel estimation. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S1.I1.i1}

    ::: {#S1.I1.i1.p1 .ltx_para}
    We propose Co-BM-DSP based on the designed preambles to estimate SOP, frequency offset, SPO, synchronization position, and equalizer coefficients to process the burst signal for CO-TDMA.
    :::
-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S1.I1.i2}

    ::: {#S1.I1.i2.p1 .ltx_para}
    The feasibility of the proposed Co-BM-DSP based on the 10ns designed preambles with 320 symbols is experimentally verified by 200G CO-TDMA based on a 32GBaud PDM-16QAM signal.
    :::
-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S1.I1.i3}

    ::: {#S1.I1.i3.p1 .ltx_para}
    The channel estimation based on the minimum-mean-square-error (MMSE) criterion and the designed 3$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}64 CAZAC symbols is theoretically proven to have a unique solution for ensuring reliability.
    :::
:::

### [[II-A]{#S2.SS1.4.1.1 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Designed Preambles for CO-TDMA]{#S2.SS1.5.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-a-designed-preambles-for-co-tdma .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

Before the payload symbols, the designed preambles consist of two parts: Preamble A and Preamble B. In addition, one pilot symbol is inserted into every 32 payload symbols for the pilot-based carrier phase recovery (CPR). Preamble B uses three sets of 64 CAZAC symbols for estimating equalizer coefficients and the synchronization position. The three sets of 64 CAZAC symbols are multiplied by the coefficients of $1$, $1$, and $- 1$ for the X polarization. For the Y polarization, the coefficients are $- 1$, $1$, and $1$. Fig. [[2]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ II Designed Preambles and Co-BM-DSP ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}(c) shows the spectra of Preamble A where the frequency tones are located at 8GHz and 16GHz for X polarization and Y polarization when the $R_{\text{s}}$ is set to 32GBaud, respectively. Preambles $\mathbf{B}_{X}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{Y}$ have ideal auto-correlation, which are appropriate for the frame synchronization. Meanwhile, their time-domain and frequency-domain envelopes are constant, making Preamble B suitable for feed-forward channel estimation.

### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.4.1.1 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Co-BM-DSP Based on Designed Preambles]{#S2.SS2.5.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-b-co-bm-dsp-based-on-designed-preambles .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
Figure [[3]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S2.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ II-A Designed Preambles for CO-TDMA ‣ II Designed Preambles and Co-BM-DSP ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref} shows the block diagram of Co-BM-DSP based on the designed preambles at the receiver side. Based on Preamble A, frame detection, coarse frequency offset estimation (FOE), and one-tap SOP estimation can be implemented. After the matched filtering and chromatic dispersion compensation (CDC), the feed-forward SPO estimation and fine FOE are implemented by Preamble A to initialize the Godard-based timing recovery and finely compensate frequency offset, respectively. Based on Preamble B, frame synchronization and feed-forward channel estimation can be realized. MMSE and zero-forcing (ZF) algorithms can implement the feed-forward channel estimation to calculate the tap coefficients. After initializing the tap coefficients calculated by the proposed channel estimation, the tap coefficients of 2$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}2 multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) equalizer are updated by the feedback decision-direct least mean square (DD-LMS) algorithm. Finally, after the pilot-based CPR, the recovered 16QAM is de-mapped into bits. Next, the Co-BM-DSP algorithms will be introduced in detail.
:::

:::: {#S2.SS2.SSS1 .section .ltx_subsubsection}
#### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.SSS1.4.1.1 .ltx_text}1 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsubsection}Frame Detection and Coarse FOE Based on Preamble A {#ii-b1-frame-detection-and-coarse-foe-based-on-preamble-a .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsubsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS1.p1 .ltx_para}
The received burst signal is first sliced within a sliding window to detect the burst frame. Then the sliced signals are transferred to the frequency domain to search two symmetric frequency tones of Preamble A. Once two symmetric frequency tones are recognized, the arrival time of one burst frame is confirmed. After the frame detection, the frequency offset should be estimated and compensated to ensure the signal spectrum within the passband of the matched filter. Based on Preamble A, the FOE can be implemented by obtaining the center frequency offset between two symmetric tones. However, discrete Fourier transform (DFT) size determines the frequency resolution, limiting the accuracy of FOE based on Preamble A. Therefore, the FOE based on Preamble A is still coarse, and a finer FOE is required. More details about the frame detection and coarse FOE based on Preamble A can be found in References \[[26](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib26){.ltx_ref}, [27](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref}\].
:::

According to the Jones matrix \[[28](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib28){.ltx_ref}, [29](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib29){.ltx_ref}\], the received Preamble A of the X polarization and Y polarization can be modeled as

        $\left\lbrack \begin{array}{ll}                                                                                          [(1)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
        R_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} & R_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} \\                                                                       
        R_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} & R_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}}                                                                          
        \end{array} \right\rbrack =$                                                                                             
        $\begin{bmatrix}

        \end{bmatrix}$                                                                                                           
  -- -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------

where $\alpha$ is the power split ratio and $\theta$ is the relative phase difference between the X polarization and Y polarization \[[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib30){.ltx_ref}, [31](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#bib.bib31){.ltx_ref}\]. The $\alpha$ can be calculated by

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $$\frac{\left| R_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} \right|^{2} + \left| R_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} \right|^{2}}{\left| R_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} \right|^{2} + \left| R_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} \right|^{2}} = \frac{1 - \alpha}{\alpha}$$      [(2)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $| \cdot |^{2}$ denotes the power of the tones. After SOP recovery by multiplexing the inverse Jones matrix, Preamble A can be expressed as

  -- -- ------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------
        $\left\lbrack \begin{array}{ll}                            [(3)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
        S_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} & S_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} \\         
        S_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} & S_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}}            
        \end{array} \right\rbrack =$                               
        ${\begin{bmatrix}                                          
        \sqrt{1 - \alpha} & {\sqrt{\alpha}e^{- {j\theta}}} \\      
        {- {\sqrt{\alpha}e^{j\theta}}} & \sqrt{1 - \alpha}         
        \end{bmatrix} \times \left\lbrack \begin{array}{ll}        
        R_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} & R_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} \\         
        R_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} & R_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}}            
        \end{array} \right\rbrack}.$                               
  -- -- ------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------

When the $\alpha$ is confirmed, $\theta$ can be searched by maximizing the power sum of $S_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}}$ of the X polarization and $S_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}}$ of the Y polarization, which can be calculated by

  -- ------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------
     $\theta_{\text{max}} =$   $\underset{\theta}{argmax}\left( {\left| S_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} \right|^{2} + \left| S_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} \right|^{2}} \right)$      [(4)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------

where ${argmax}{( \cdot )}$ represents the set that maximizes the input variable and $0 \leq \theta < {2\pi}$.
:::

:::: {#S2.SS2.SSS3 .section .ltx_subsubsection}
#### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.SSS3.4.1.1 .ltx_text}3 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsubsection}Phase-Initialized Timing Recovery Based on Preamble A {#ii-b3-phase-initialized-timing-recovery-based-on-preamble-a .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsubsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS3.p1 .ltx_para}
After the one-tap SOP estimation and recovery, the recovered Preamble A $S_{X,{R_{\text{s}}/2}}$ and $S_{Y,{R_{\text{s}}/4}}$ can be used for the feed-forward SPO estimation for X polarization and Y polarization, respectively. Using the tone at $R_{\text{s}}/2$, the initial SPO $\tau_{0,{R_{\text{s}}/2}}$ can be estimated by

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $$\tau_{0,{R_{\text{s}}/2}} = {\frac{K}{2\pi}{\arg\left\lbrack {S{({{N/2} - {N/K/2}})}S^{\ast}{({{N/2} + {N/K/2}})}} \right\rbrack}}$$      [(5)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $K$ is the oversampling rate and $N$ is the DFT size. $\arg{( \cdot )}$ denotes calculating the angle of a complex and ${( \cdot )}^{\ast}$ represents the conjugation of a complex. Using the tone at $R_{\text{s}}/4$, the initial SPO $\tau_{0,{R_{\text{s}}/4}}$ can be estimated by

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${\tau_{0,{R_{\text{s}}/4}} = {\frac{K}{\pi}{\arg\left\lbrack {S{({{N/2} - {N/K/4}})}S^{\ast}{({{N/2} + {N/K/4}})}} \right\rbrack}}}.$$      [(6)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------

However, the estimated SPO $\tau_{0}$ includes the phase frequency response of the channel at $R_{\text{s}}/K$. When the timing error detector (TED) uses some specific frequency points, the estimated SPO using a different tone should be offset. For example, when the Godard-based TED based on frequency points near $R_{\text{s}}/2$ is employed, the estimated SPO using the tone at $R_{\text{s}}/4$ of the Y polarization requires a fixed phase offset for the TR based on Godard-based TED to achieve fast convergence, while the initial phase using the tone at $R_{\text{s}}/2$ of the X polarization does not require an offset. Finally, the estimated SPO initializes the timing recovery to reduce the convergence time.
:::

#### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.SSS4.4.1.1 .ltx_text}4 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsubsection}Fine FOE Based on Preamble A {#ii-b4-fine-foe-based-on-preamble-a .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsubsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS4.p1 .ltx_para}
The third function of Preamble A is for fine FOE. As the frequency tones, the symbols of Preamble A are periodic in the time domain. Without loss of generality, we analyze the principle of fine FOE based on Preamble A for the X polarization $t_{X}{(n)}$. The Preamble A with frequency offset can be modeled as

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{r_{X}{(n)}} = {{t_{X}{(n)}} \cdot e^{{j2\pi n\Delta f}/R_{\text{s}}}}}.$$      [(7)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

Then the fine FOE can be implemented by removing the phase of symbols and extracting the slope of phase when ${t_{X}{(n)}} = {t_{X}{({n + L})}}$ where $L$ is the period of the Preamble A for the X polarization. Therefore, the fine FOE can be expressed as

  -- ------------ ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------
     $\Delta f$   $= {\frac{R_{\text{s}}}{2\pi L} \cdot {\arg\left\lbrack {\sum\limits_{n}{{{r_{X}^{\ast}{(n)}} \cdot r_{X}}{({n + L})}}} \right\rbrack}}$                                  [(8)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
                  $= {\frac{R_{\text{s}}}{2\pi L} \cdot {\arg\left\lbrack {\sum\limits_{n}{\left| {t_{X}{(n)}} \right|^{2} \cdot e^{{j2\pi L\Delta f}/R_{\text{s}}}}} \right\rbrack}}$      
  -- ------------ ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ---------------------------------------------------------
:::

:::: {#S2.SS2.SSS5 .section .ltx_subsubsection}
#### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.SSS5.4.1.1 .ltx_text}5 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsubsection}Frame Synchronization Based on Preamble B {#ii-b5-frame-synchronization-based-on-preamble-b .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsubsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS5.p1 .ltx_para}
Frame synchronization can be implemented by calculating the timing metric based on the correlation values between transmitted and the received Preamble B. Preamble B can be expressed as $\lbrack{{\text{B}_{X}\text{B}_{X}} - \text{B}_{X}}\rbrack$ and $\lbrack{- {\text{B}_{Y}\text{B}_{Y}\text{B}_{Y}}}\rbrack$ where $\text{B}_{X}$ and $\text{B}_{Y}$ are two sets of CAZAC symbols with different seeds for X polarization and Y polarization, respectively. Preamble B can be used to acquire the synchronization position by seeking the prominent peak in the timing metric. We defined the correlation value $M_{X/Y}{(n)}$ as

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $${{M_{X/Y}{(n)}} = \frac{2{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{L_{\text{B}}}{{{r_{X/Y}{({n + i})}} \cdot B_{X/Y}^{\ast}}{(n)}}}}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{L_{\text{B}}}{|{r_{X/Y}{({n + i})}}|}^{2}}}.$$      [(9)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $L_{\text{B}}$ denotes the length of $B_{X}$ and $B_{Y}$. To obtain the timing metric $P_{X/Y}{(n)}$, the delay of 0, $L_{\text{B}}$, and $2L_{\text{B}}$ symbols are added to $M_{X/Y}{(n)}$, respectively. The $M_{X/Y}{(n)}$, $M_{X/Y}{({n + L_{\text{B}}})}$, and $M_{X/Y}{({n + {2L_{\text{B}}}})}$ are multiplied by the coefficients of the Preamble B, and stack their outputs over to obtain the timing metric $P_{X}{(n)}$. In conclusion, the timing metric $P_{X}{(n)}$ for the X polarization can be calculated by

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${{P_{X}{(n)}} = \left| {{{M_{X}{(n)}} + {M_{X}{({n + L_{\text{B}}})}}} - {M_{X}{({n + {2L_{\text{B}}}})}}} \right|^{2}}.$$      [(10)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

The timing metric $P_{Y}{(n)}$ for the Y polarization can be calculated by

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${{P_{Y}{(n)}} = \left| {{- {M_{Y}{(n)}}} + {M_{Y}{({n + L_{\text{B}}})}} + {M_{Y}{({n + {2L_{\text{B}}}})}}} \right|^{2}}.$$      [(11)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

The highest peaks of $P_{X}{(n)}$ and $P_{Y}{(n)}$ indicate the synchronization positions of the X polarization and Y polarization, respectively.
:::

#### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.SSS6.4.1.1 .ltx_text}6 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsubsection}MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B {#ii-b6-mmse-channel-estimation-based-on-preamble-b .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsubsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS6.p1 .ltx_para}
Next, we will introduce the principle of channel estimation based on Preamble B. The mean square error (MSE) between the output of the MIMO equalizer and the transmitted signal can be expressed as

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${J_{X}{(\omega)}} = {E\left\lbrack \left| {{{W_{XX}R_{X}} + {W_{XY}R_{Y}}} - T_{X}} \right|^{2} \right\rbrack}$$      [(12)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${J_{Y}{(\omega)}} = {E\left\lbrack \left| {{{W_{YX}R_{X}} + {W_{YY}R_{Y}}} - T_{Y}} \right|^{2} \right\rbrack}$$      [(13)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $E{( \cdot )}$ is the expectation operation. $W_{XX}$, $W_{XY}$, $W_{YX}$, and $W_{YY}$ are the frequency-domain tap coefficients of the MIMO equalizer. $R_{X/Y}$ and $T_{X/Y}$ are the received and transmitted Preamble B of the X polarization or Y polarization in the frequency domain, respectively.
:::

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS6.p2 .ltx_para}
The channel estimation based on the MMSE criterion minimizes $J_{X}{(\omega)}$ and $J_{Y}{(\omega)}$ to obtain the tap coefficients of the MIMO equalizer. By separately taking the partial derivatives of $J_{X}{(\omega)}$ for $W_{XX}$ and $W_{XY}$, a system of binary first-order equations can be expressed as

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${E\left\lbrack {\left( {{{W_{XX}R_{X}} + {W_{XY}R_{Y}}} - T_{X}} \right) \cdot R_{X}^{\ast}} \right\rbrack} = 0$$      [(14)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${{E\left\lbrack {\left( {{{W_{XX}R_{X}} + {W_{XY}R_{Y}}} - T_{X}} \right) \cdot R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right\rbrack} = 0},$$      [(15)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

which can be solved to obtain $W_{XX}$ and $W_{XX}$ for minimizing $J_{X}{(\omega)}$. It is worth noting that the system of binary first-order equations should have a unique solution. Owing to the different coefficients on the elements of Preamble B, only a unique solution exists, which is proven in Appendix A. Therefore, $W_{XX}$ and $W_{XY}$ can be estimated by

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $$W_{XX} = \frac{{{E\left( {T_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {T_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}}}{{{E\left( {R_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {R_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}}}$$      [(16)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${W_{XY} = \frac{{{E\left( {T_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {T_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}}}{{{E\left( {R_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {R_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}}}}.$$      [(17)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

Similarly, by separately taking the partial derivatives of $J_{Y}{(\omega)}$ for $W_{YX}$ and $W_{YY}$, a system of binary first order equations can be expressed as

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${E\left\lbrack {\left( {{{W_{YX}R_{X}} + {W_{YY}R_{Y}}} - T_{Y}} \right) \cdot R_{X}^{\ast}} \right\rbrack} = 0$$      [(18)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${{E\left\lbrack {\left( {{{W_{YX}R_{X}} + {W_{YY}R_{Y}}} - T_{Y}} \right) \cdot R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right\rbrack} = 0}.$$      [(19)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

which can be solved to obtain $W_{XX}$ and $W_{XX}$ for minimizing $J_{Y}{(\omega)}$. Therefore, $W_{XX}$ and $W_{XX}$ can be estimated by

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $$W_{YX} = \frac{{{E\left( {T_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {T_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}}}{{{E\left( {R_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {R_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}}}$$      [(20)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${W_{YY} = \frac{{{E\left( {T_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {T_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}}}{{{E\left( {R_{X}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}} - {{E\left( {R_{X}R_{Y}^{\ast}} \right)}{E\left( {R_{Y}R_{X}^{\ast}} \right)}}}}.$$      [(21)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
:::

#### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.SSS7.4.1.1 .ltx_text}7 ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsubsection}ZF Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B {#ii-b7-zf-channel-estimation-based-on-preamble-b .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsubsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.SSS7.p1 .ltx_para}
Owing to the one-tap SOP estimation and recovery, there is almost no crosstalk between the X polarization and Y polarization, which means that the frequency responses $H_{YX}$ and $H_{XY}$ of the crosstalk between X polarization and Y polarization can be approximate to zeros. Therefore, the Preamble B can also be used for ZF channel estimation with less computational complexity than MMSE channel estimation. Without the consideration of noise, the Preamble B after the SOP recovery can be represented as

  -- -------------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------------
     $\left\lbrack \begin{array}{l}   $\approx {\begin{bmatrix}                               [(22)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
     S_{X} \\                         H_{XX} & 0 \\                                           
     S_{Y}                            0 & H_{YY}                                              
     \end{array} \right\rbrack$       \end{bmatrix} \times \left\lbrack \begin{array}{l}      
                                      T_{X} \\                                                
                                      T_{Y}                                                   
                                      \end{array} \right\rbrack}$                             
  -- -------------------------------- ---------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------------

where $H_{XX}$ and $H_{YY}$ are the frequency response of the X polarization and Y polarization, respectively. The $W_{XX}$ and $W_{YY}$ can be calculated by

  -- ------------------ --------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------------
     $\begin{bmatrix}   $= \begin{bmatrix}                         [(23)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
     W_{XX} \\          {1/H_{XX}} \\                              
     W_{YY}             {1/H_{YY}}                                 
     \end{bmatrix}$     \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix}      
                        {T_{X}/S_{X}} \\                           
                        {T_{Y}/S_{Y}}                              
                        \end{bmatrix}$                             
  -- ------------------ --------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------------

where three sets of tap coefficients can be estimated using the Preamble B, which can be averaged to obtain a more accurate set. The tap coefficients estimated by MMSE or ZF initialize the MIMO equalizer to reduce the convergence time.

## [IV ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Experimental Results]{#S4.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#iv-experimental-results .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S4.p1 .ltx_para}
Figures [[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S2.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ II-B7 ZF Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ II-B Co-BM-DSP Based on Designed Preambles ‣ II Designed Preambles and Co-BM-DSP ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}(a) and (b) show the spectra of the received Preamble A without and with the one-tap SOP estimation and recovery, respectively. Before the one-tap SOP recovery, the $R_{\text{s}}/2$ and $R_{\text{s}}/4$ tones interfere between the X polarization and Y polarization. After the one-tap SOP estimation and recovery, most power of the $R_{\text{s}}/2$ tones appears at the X polarization, and most power of the $R_{\text{s}}/4$ tones appears at the Y polarization. Fig. [[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S2.F5 "Figure 5 ‣ II-B7 ZF Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ II-B Co-BM-DSP Based on Designed Preambles ‣ II Designed Preambles and Co-BM-DSP ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}(c) shows the power sum of two frequency tones at $R_{\text{s}}/2$ for the X polarization and at $R_{\text{s}}/4$ for the Y polarization versus $\theta$ in Eq. With the parameter $\theta$ in the range from 0 to $2\pi$, the power sum is a sine function. ## [V ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Conclusions]{#S5.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#v-conclusions .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S5.p1 .ltx_para}
This paper proposes a Co-BM-DSP based on an approximately 10ns designed preamble for upstream reception of 200G CO-TDMA using 32GBaud PDM-16QAM. The designed preamble can be decomposed into a preamble of frequency tones with 128 symbols and a preamble of 192 CAZAC symbols. The frequency tones can be used for SOP, frequency offset, and SPO estimation. The CAZAC symbols can be employed for estimating frame synchronization and tap coefficients of the MIMO equalizer. Based on the designed preamble, the SPO of timing recovery and tap coefficients of the MIMO equalizer can be estimated quickly by the feed-forward algorithms and initialized to put the feedback algorithms under the right status. It is worth noting that our offline experiments in the Lab are relatively ideal. The preamble length can be properly increased to ensure the performance of the actual scenarios. In conclusion, the proposed Co-BM-DSP with a compact preamble makes it more possible to be applied in the future CO-TDMA.
:::

([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E27 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. ([[28]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E28 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) have a unique solution. If all the items are equal to zero in Eq. ([[29]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E29 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) due to

  -- ---------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $${{R_{Xi}R_{Yj}} = {R_{Yi}R_{Xj}}},$$      [(31)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

the determinant is equal to zero. Eq. ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E27 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. ([[28]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E28 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) may have infinite solutions or no solution. To confirm which one is right, we should consider

  -- --------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------------
     $\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{3}$   ${R_{\text{X}i}R_{\text{X}i}^{\ast}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{3}{R_{\text{Y}i}^{\ast}T_{\text{X}i}}}} - {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{3}{R_{\text{X}i}^{\ast}T_{\text{X}i}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{3}{R_{\text{X}i}R_{\text{Y}i}^{\ast}}}}}$      [(32)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equationgroup .ltx_align_right}
     $=$                         ${\underset{0}{\underbrace{\left( {{R_{\text{X}⁢1}⁢R_{\text{Y}⁢2}}-{R_{\text{Y}⁢1}⁢R_{\text{X}⁢2}}} \right)^{\ast}}}\left( {{R_{\text{X}1}T_{\text{X}2}} - {T_{\text{X}1}R_{\text{X}2}}} \right)} +$                                    
                                 ${\underset{0}{\underbrace{\left( {{R_{\text{X}⁢1}⁢R_{\text{Y}⁢3}}-{R_{\text{Y}⁢1}⁢R_{\text{X}⁢3}}} \right)^{\ast}}}\left( {{R_{\text{X}1}T_{\text{X}3}} - {T_{\text{X}1}R_{\text{X}3}}} \right)} +$                                    
                                 ${\underset{0}{\underbrace{\left( {{R_{\text{X}⁢2}⁢R_{\text{Y}⁢3}}-{R_{\text{Y}⁢2}⁢R_{\text{X}⁢3}}} \right)^{\ast}}}\left( {{R_{\text{X}2}T_{\text{X}3}} - {T_{\text{X}2}R_{\text{X}3}}} \right)},$                                     
  -- --------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------------

which is equal to zero. Therefore, there are infinite solutions for Eq. ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E27 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. ([[28]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E28 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}). In conclusion, there are only two cases for solving Eq. ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E27 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. ([[28]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E28 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}), including unique and infinite solutions.

Using the designed Preamble B, the determinant is not equal to zero due to ${R_{X1}R_{Y2}} \neq {R_{Y1}R_{X2}}$ and ${R_{X3}R_{Y2}} \neq {R_{Y3}R_{X2}}$. Therefore, the Eq. ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E27 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. However, without the coefficients of $\lbrack 1,1,{- 1}\rbrack$ for the X polarization and $\lbrack{- 1},1,1\rbrack$ for the Y polarization, the determinant and Eq. ([[32]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E32 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) are equal to zero, which would lead to infinite solutions of $\mathbf{W}_{\text{XX}}$ and $\mathbf{W}_{\text{XY}}$ for Eq. ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E27 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. ([[28]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#A1.E28 "In Appendix A Proof of The Unique Solutions Using MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}). A similar proof can be applied to Eq. ([[18]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S2.E18 "In II-B6 MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ II-B Co-BM-DSP Based on Designed Preambles ‣ II Designed Preambles and Co-BM-DSP ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) and Eq. ([[19]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10080v1#S2.E19 "In II-B6 MMSE Channel Estimation Based on Preamble B ‣ II-B Co-BM-DSP Based on Designed Preambles ‣ II Designed Preambles and Co-BM-DSP ‣ Burst-Mode Digital Signal Processing for Coherent Optical Time-Division Multiple Access"){.ltx_ref}) to solving the $\mathbf{W}_{\text{YX}}$ and $\mathbf{W}_{\text{YY}}$. In conclusion, MMSE channel estimation based on the designed Preamble B is theoretically proven to have unique solutions for obtaining the tap coefficients, ensuring the reliability of the Co-BM-DSP.
