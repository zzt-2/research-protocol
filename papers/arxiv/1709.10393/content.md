::: {#abstract1 .ltx_abstract}
###### Abstract {#abstract .ltx_title .ltx_title_abstract}

In this paper, achievable information rates (AIR) for fiber optical communications are discussed. It is shown that AIRs such as the mutual information and generalized mutual information are good design metrics for coded optical systems. The theoretical predictions of AIRs are compared to the performance of modern codes including low-parity density check (LDPC) and polar codes. Two different computation methods for these AIRs are also discussed: Monte-Carlo integration and Gauss-Hermite quadrature. Closed-form ready-to-use approximations for such computations are provided for arbitrary constellations and the multidimensional AWGN channel. The computation of AIRs in optical experiments and simulations is also discussed.
:::

## [I ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Introduction and Motivation]{#S1.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#i-introduction-and-motivation .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S1.p1 .ltx_para}
Through a series of revolutionary technological advances, optical transmission systems have supported the Internet's traffic growth for decades \[[1](#bib.bib1){.ltx_ref}\]. Most of the large bandwidth available in fiber systems is in use \[[2](#bib.bib2){.ltx_ref}\], however, it seems like the capacity of the optical core network cannot keep up with the traffic growth \[[3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}, [4](#bib.bib4){.ltx_ref}\]. This makes information-theoretic analyses of optical fiber systems very important so as to maximize the information rates and spectral efficiencies of the nonlinear optical channel.
:::

::: {#S1.p2 .ltx_para}
The optical fiber channel is effectively band-limited by the fiber loss profile and the operating range of the optical amplifiers \[[1](#bib.bib1){.ltx_ref}\]. Because of this, the need of designing bandwidth-efficient transceivers is an active area of research. The most natural way of achieving this is via multi-level modulation combined with forward error correction (FEC), a combination known as coded modulation (CM). Different coded modulation *flavors* exist, the most popular ones being trellis coded modulation \[[5](#bib.bib5){.ltx_ref}\], bit-interleaved coded modulation (BICM) \[[6](#bib.bib6){.ltx_ref}, [7](#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref}, [8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, [9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, [10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}\], and MLC \[[11](#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}, [12](#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}\]. FEC is usually either soft-decision (SD-FEC) or hard-decision (HD-FEC), which refers to the type of information passed to the FEC decoder: soft bits or hard bits, respectively.
:::

::: {#S1.p3 .ltx_para}
CM has become a key technology for fiber-optical communications, as shown in Fig. [[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S1.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ I Introduction and Motivation ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}. This figure shows the number of occurrences of "$M$QAM" in the OFC proceedings in the last 15 years. Although the number of occurrences is a very rough metric (and can certainly be improved), this figure does show a clear trend: QPSK and 16QAM are here to stay, and 64QAM and 256QAM are slowly becoming more important.
:::

<figure id="S1.F1" class="ltx_figure">

<figcaption><span class="ltx_tag ltx_tag_figure">Fig. 1: </span>Number of appearances of “<span class="math inline"><em>M</em></span>QAM” in OFC proceedings 2003–2017.</figcaption>
</figure>

::: {#S1.p4 .ltx_para}
Ungerboeck's celebrated trellis-coded modulation \[[5](#bib.bib5){.ltx_ref}\] was very popular because the receiver could find the most likely coded sequence using a single low-complexity decoder, that exploited the nonbinary (NB) trellis structure of the code. With the advent of powerful SD-FEC (such low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes \[[13](#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref}\], turbo codes \[[14](#bib.bib14){.ltx_ref}\], and polar codes \[[15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}\]), however, modern fiber optical CM transceivers use a receiver based on *binary* FEC. When only one code is used, the scheme is known as BICM \[[6](#bib.bib6){.ltx_ref}, [7](#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref}, [8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, [9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, [10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}\]. When multiple codes are used, the system is called multilevel codes (MLC) \[[11](#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}, [12](#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}\]. In MLC, two decoding strategies are possible: Multi-stage decoding (MLC-MSD) \[[12](#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}, Sec. II\] and parallel, independent decoding of the individual levels (MLC-PDL) \[[12](#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}, Sec. VI-B\]. Both BICM and MLC-PDL are pragmatic (but suboptimal) approaches to CM where the detection is split into two stages: demap the bits independently, and then decode.
:::

::: {#S1.p5 .ltx_para}
Until a few years ago, pre-FEC bit-error rate (BER), symbol error rate (SER), Q-factor, and error vector magnitude (EVM) were the standard performance metrics in the optical communications community. Recently, however, this paradigm has been changing, and achievable information rates (AIRs) are becoming more popular. AIRs indicate the number of information bits per symbol that can be reliably transmitted through the channel and are at the core of Shannon's celebrated concept of *channel capacity* \[[16](#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref}\]. Because of the definition of AIRs, one of the key advantages of using them as performance metrics is that they are inherently related to FEC. Unlike uncoded metrics like the pre-FEC BER, SER, EVM, or Q-factor, AIRs give an indication of the amount of *information bits* that can be reliably transferred through a channel. While uncoded metrics are related to bits before and after the demapper, AIRs deal with the information bits before and after FEC.
:::

::: {#S1.p6 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we discuss the use of AIRs as a tool to design CM transceivers with both NB and binary FEC. By means of simple examples, AIRs are shown to be very powerful design metrics. AIRs will be used to allow fair comparisons of different constellations, DSP, decoding and nonlinearity compensation techniques, and also for post-FEC error prediction.[^1^[[^1^ [1]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} AIRs can also be used to characterize optical transceivers, as done in \[[17](#bib.bib17){.ltx_ref}\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote1 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} An AIR for CM based on NB codes or MLC-MSD is the mutual information (MI) \[[16](#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref}, [18](#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref}, [19](#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}, [20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\]. For binary FEC (with BICM or with MLC-PDL), however, the relevant quantity is the GMI \[[7](#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref}, [8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, [9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, [10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}\]. In this paper we consider equally likely symbols only, however, the extension of GMI to nonuniform symbols can be done, as shown in \[[21](#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}, Sec. VI\].
:::

::: {#S1.p7 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we extend our results in \[[22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}\] by also considering polar coded modulation with multilevel coding (MLC). We also give general ready-to-use expressions for approximating AIRs for multidimensional modulation formats. The paper is structured as follows. In Sec. [[II]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2 "II General Aspects of AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}, we discuss general aspects of AIRs. In Sec. [[III]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3 "III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}, we present an information-theoretic treatment of AIRs. In Sec. [[IV]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S4 "IV Computation Methods for AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}, computational aspects of AIRs are discussed.
:::
:::

::: {#S2 .section .ltx_section}
## [II ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[General Aspects of AIRs]{#S2.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#ii-general-aspects-of-airs .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S2.p1 .ltx_para}
Achievable information rates (AIRs) are information-theoretic quantities that are, by definition, linked to the amount of reliable information that can be transmitted through a given channel. This section gives examples of how AIRs can be used to predict key metrics of digital communication systems. At the end of the section, we highlight the underlying assumption of such an AIR analysis and the corresponding limitations.
:::

::: {#S2.SS1 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[II-A]{#S2.SS1.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Three Applications of AIRs]{#S2.SS1.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-a-three-applications-of-airs .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S2.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
In the following, applications of AIRs are are shown via three examples. The first use case is to predict the maximum throughput of an AWGN channel. Next, AIRs are investigated to compare the maximum reach for different modulation formats and DSP techniques in optical fiber transmission. In this context, a comparison of AIRs to LDPC and polar codes is performed. Finally, the accuracy of using MI and GMI for predicting the post-FEC BER is presented. In all cases, results with FEC encoding and decoding are included to show that the throughput of these coded systems follows the AIR predictions.
:::

::: {#S2.SS2 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[II-B]{#S2.SS2.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Limitations]{#S2.SS2.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-b-limitations .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S2.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
Some important differences of AIRs and realistic FEC schemes must be considered that can limit the applicability of AIRs as performance predictors and decoding thresholds. For example, AIRs such as MI and GMI inherently assume a capacity-achieving FEC code with ideal maximum-likelihood (ML) decoding for which the complexity grows exponentially with the block length of the block code \[[36](#bib.bib36){.ltx_ref}, Sec. 5-1\]. Decoding of almost all modern codes is performed with suboptimal low-complexity decoding algorithms (such as the sum-product algorithm \[[37](#bib.bib37){.ltx_ref}\] for LDPC codes).[^6^[[^6^ [6]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Note that successive cancellation decoding for polar codes is ideal in the sense of achieving capacity. However, it does not perform well for short and moderate block lengths. The coding gap can be decreased by increasing the block length or by performing list decoding.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote6 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} When MI or GMI is used as decoding threshold for a FEC with a suboptimal decoding algorithm, trapping sets that result in an error floor must be treated with care. Such an error floor is the result of the considered code and decoding algorithm, and an AIR analysis with MI and GMI as it is carried out in this paper does not include this effect. Lastly, MI and GMI can be used as decoding thresholds only when the encoder and decoder pair in question is universal. This has been discussed in detail in \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, Secs. II-C and V\].
:::

::: {#S2.SS2.p2 .ltx_para}
A significant performance difference of polar and LDPC codes to the AIRs exists because MI and GMI are asymptotic limits for infinitely long codewords. As this requirement is clearly impossible to fulfill for any practical FEC, the coding gap between AIR and FEC performance is increased. Depending on the application, it might be sometimes more insightful to include finite-length constraints in the analysis, for example via density evolution or via finite block length regime bounds (see for example \[[38](#bib.bib38){.ltx_ref}\]).
:::

::: {#S2.SS2.p3 .ltx_para}
Another inherent assumption of the concatenated FEC schemes in this paper is that ideal interleavers are included between the inner and outer FEC. These interleaving blocks remove any error bursts that occur during transmission, which is a crucial assumption for applying the HD-FEC threshold as it is done in this paper. Furthermore, the results presented in Fig. [[6]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F6 "Fig. 6 ‣ Example 3 (Post-FEC Performance Prediction) ‣ II-A Three Applications of AIRs ‣ II General Aspects of AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref} (as well as those in \[[39](#bib.bib39){.ltx_ref}\]) were generated using a random interleaver, i.e., a random interleaving permutation was generated for every transmitted codeword. This is in contrast to the results in \[[40](#bib.bib40){.ltx_ref}\], where no interleaver was used (the code bits from the LDPC code were cyclically mapped to the modulation format). The difference between these two approaches is that the former (the one used here) is practically less relevant, however, the obtained prediction is more accurate. These results should therefore be understood as the performance prediction for the code ensemble generated by the random realizations of the interleaver. On the other hand, by not using an interleaver (as done in \[[40](#bib.bib40){.ltx_ref}\]), the post-FEC BER prediction is slightly worse for low code rates, but the results are highly practical. We conjecture that a very good trade-off between practical relevance and error prediction can be obtained if one single interleaver permutation is used all the time, as long as that permutation is randomly generated.[^7^[[^7^ [7]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Another (less practical) alternative would be to use one interleaver that covers multiple LDPC codewords.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote7 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}
:::

::: {#S2.SS2.p4 .ltx_para}
As discussed in Sec. [[III-D]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.SS4 "III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}, if the LLRs are *mismatched* to the communication channel or computed in an approximate manner, for example with the max-log approximation \[[41](#bib.bib41){.ltx_ref}\], lower AIRs can be obtained. This leads to the idea of correcting LLRs, which can also be connected to improving the performance of the decoder. For more details on this, we refer the reader to \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Ch. 7\] and references therein.
:::

### [[III-A]{#S3.SS1.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Coded Modulation]{#S3.SS1.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-a-coded-modulation .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
Coded modulation (CM) comes in different *flavors*, the most popular ones being TCM, NB-CM, MLC, and BICM. CM for optical communications is nowadays a well-established technique for spectrally efficient transmission. In this section, we focus on MLC and BICM due to their practical relevance. The FEC decoder used in CM also comes in two flavors: hard-decision (HD) and soft-decision (SD) decoding. In this paper, and due to its importance in next-generation optical transceivers, we only consider SD-FEC. For a comparison of AIRs between HD- and SD-FEC, we refer the reader to \[[19](#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}\] and references therein.[^8^[[^8^ [8]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Note that the AIRs for *nonbinary* HD FEC studied in \[[19](#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}\] are not achieved by standard Hamming-distance-based nonbinary HD FEC decoders, but instead, by codes that use some soft-decision for decoding. This was recently shown in \[[42](#bib.bib42){.ltx_ref}\], which was initially correctly pointed out in \[[19](#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}, Footnote 10\]. For more explanations on this, we refer the reader to \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Sec. 3.4\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote8 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p2 .ltx_para}
In general, the optical channel suffer from intersymbol and interpolarization interference (see inner part of Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). Most (if not all) optical receivers compensate for these effects, however, residual intersymbol interference (due to, e.g., Kerr nonlinearities after dispersion compensation) are typically neglected[^9^[[^9^ [9]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} When taking into account this residual interference, improved AIRs for fiber optics have been studied in, e.g.,\[[43](#bib.bib43){.ltx_ref}, [44](#bib.bib44){.ltx_ref}\]]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote9 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}. Furthermore, each polarization is detected independently, and the soft information on the coded bits is calculated ignoring residual correlation between symbols in time. This is highlighted in Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}, where a memoryless demapper is included at the receiver. The resulting "information-theoretic channel" is also shown in this figure, which can be interpreted as an "average" channel. This channel is fully characterized by the conditional PDF $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{Y}} \middle| {\mathbf{X}}) \right.$, where $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ are the transmitted and received symbols, respectively. We will return to these memoryless assumptions in Sec. [[V]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5 "V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}.
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p3 .ltx_para}
The transmitted symbols $\mathbf{X}$ are assumed to be multidimensional (MD) symbols with $N$ complex dimensions (or equivalently, with $2\hspace{0pt}N$ real dimensions) drawn uniformly from a discrete constellation $\mathcal{X}$ with cardinality $M = 2^{m} = {|\mathcal{X}|}$. The noisy received symbols are MD symbols with $N$ complex dimensions, i.e., ${\mathbf{Y}} \in {\mathbb{C}}^{N}$. We consider constellation points ${\mathbf{x}}_{i} = {\lbrack{x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\ldots,x_{i,N}}\rbrack} \in {\mathbb{C}}^{N}$ with ${i = 1},2,\ldots,M$. The difference between two symbols is defined as the vector ${\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}j} \triangleq {{\mathbf{x}}_{i} - {\mathbf{x}}_{j}}$, and the uniform input PMF by ${P_{\mathbf{X}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{x}})}} = {1/M}$.[^10^[[^10^ [10]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Notation: Random variables are denoted by capital letters $X$ and random vectors by boldface letters $\mathbf{X}$. Their corresponding realizations are denoted by $x$ and $\mathbf{x}$. The inner product between two vectors is denoted by $\left\langle {{\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\mathbf{x}}_{2}} \right\rangle$. Expectations are denoted by ${\mathbb{E}}{\lbrack \cdot \rbrack}$ and the squared Euclidean norm is defined as ${\|{\mathbf{X}}\|}^{2} = {{|X_{1}|}^{2} + {|X_{2}|}^{2} + \ldots + {|X_{N}|}^{2}}$. Conditional probability density functions (PDFs) are denoted by $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.$. The imaginary unit is $\jmath \triangleq \sqrt{- 1}$.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote10 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} The most popular case in fiber optical communications is $N = 2$, which corresponds to coherent communications using two polarizations of the light (4 real dimensions).
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p4 .ltx_para}
Throughout this paper we consider four different CM structures based on SD-FEC. These four alternatives are shown in Table [[II]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T2 "TABLE II ‣ III-A Coded Modulation ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}. The first one is NB-CM where a NB FEC is concatenated with a nonbinary modulation format (see Fig. [[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ Example 3 (Post-FEC Performance Prediction) ‣ II-A Three Applications of AIRs ‣ II General Aspects of AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and the second one is MLC-MSD (see Fig. [[2]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ Example 1 (Throughput Prediction) ‣ II-A Three Applications of AIRs ‣ II General Aspects of AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). An AIR for these two cases is the MI. The third and fourth alternatives are BICM (see Fig. [[3]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F3 "Fig. 3 ‣ Example 1 (Throughput Prediction) ‣ II-A Three Applications of AIRs ‣ II General Aspects of AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and MLC-PDL, for which the GMI is an AIR.
:::

::: {#S3.SS2 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[III-B]{#S3.SS2.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Channel Capacity]{#S3.SS2.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-b-channel-capacity .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
A *coding scheme* consists of a codebook, an encoder, and a decoder. The codebook is the set of codewords that can be transmitted through the channel, where each codeword is a sequence of symbols. The encoder is a one-to-one mapping between the information sequences and codewords. The decoder is a deterministic rule that maps the noisy channel observations onto an information sequence.
:::

::: {#S3.SS2.p2 .ltx_para}
A rate, in bits per MD symbol (or simply bit/sym), is said to be *achievable* at a given block length $L$ and for a given average error probability $\varepsilon$ if there exists a coding scheme whose average error probability is below $\varepsilon$. The *channel capacity* introduced by C. E. Shannon in 1948 \[[16](#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref}\] is the largest AIR for which a coding scheme with vanishing error probability exists, in the limit of large block length. In simple words, the channel capacity represents the number of bits that can be reliably ''pushed'' through a given channel. Shannon also proved that the channel capacity cannot be exceeded, i.e., rates above the channel capacity cannot be reliable transmitted.[^11^[[^11^ [11]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} This is known in the information theory literature as the converse of the channel coding theorem which was proven in its strong form in \[[47](#bib.bib47){.ltx_ref}\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote11 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} For a more detailed and precise description of these concepts in the context of optical communications, we refer the reader to \[[48](#bib.bib48){.ltx_ref}\]. In what follows, we discuss two AIRs: MI and GMI.
:::
:::

::: {#S3.SS3 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[III-C]{#S3.SS3.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Mutual Information]{#S3.SS3.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-c-mutual-information .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS3.p1 .ltx_para}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the binary codebook used for transmission and $\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}}$ denote the transmitted codewords as

  -- ---------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     ${\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}} = \begin{bmatrix}      [(1)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
     c_{1,1} & c_{1,2} & \ldots & c_{1,L} \\           
      & & \ddots & \\                                  
     c_{m,1} & c_{m,2} & \ldots & c_{m,L}              
     \end{bmatrix}},$                                  
  -- ---------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where the block length (in symbols) is $L$.
:::

::: {#S3.SS3.p2 .ltx_para}
Furthermore, let ${\mathbf{B}} = {\lbrack{B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}}\rbrack}$ be a random vector representing the transmitted bits $\lbrack{c_{1,l},\ldots,c_{m,l}}\rbrack$ at any time instant $l$, which are mapped to the corresponding symbol $X_{l} \in \mathcal{X}$ with ${l = 1},2,\ldots,L$. Assuming a memoryless channel, the optimal symbol-wise (SW) ML receiver chooses the transmitted codeword based on an observed sequence $\underset{¯}{\mathbf{y}} = {\lbrack{{\mathbf{y}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{y}}_{L}}\rbrack}$ according to the rule

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------
     ${\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}}}^{\text{sw}} \triangleq \operatorname{argmax}_{\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}} \in \mathcal{C}}\sum\limits_{l = 1}^{L}\log f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\left. ({\mathbf{y}}_{l} \middle| c_{1,l},\ldots,c_{m,l}) \right.$      [(2)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------

where ${f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{b}}) \right.} = {f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.}$ is the channel law.
:::

::: {#S3.SS3.p3 .ltx_para}
Shannon's channel coding theorem states that reliable transmission with the SW decoder in ([[2]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E2 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is possible at arbitrarily low error probability if the combined rate of the binary encoder and mapper (in information bit per MD symbol) is below the MI $I{({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})}$, i.e., if ${R_{c}\hspace{0pt}m} \leq {I{({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})}}$. In other words, for any memoryless channel, the largest achievable rate is the MI defined as

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $I = {I{({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})}} \triangleq {{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\frac{f_{{\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{X}})}}{f_{\mathbf{Y}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}\hspace{0pt}f_{\mathbf{X}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{X}})}}} \right\rbrack}$      [(3)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------

where ${\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}}$ denotes the expectation with respect to both $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$.
:::

::: {#S3.SS3.p4 .ltx_para}
The MI in ([[3]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E3 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) for discrete constellations and equally likely symbols can be expressed as

  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $I$   $\phantom{} = {{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\frac{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{Y}} \middle| {\mathbf{X}}) \right.}{f_{\mathbf{Y}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}}} \right\rbrack}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         [(4)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
           $\phantom{} = {\frac{1}{M}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{M}{\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.\hspace{0pt}\log_{2}\hspace{0pt}\frac{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.}{\frac{1}{M}\hspace{0pt}{\sum_{j = 1}^{M}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{j}) \right.}}}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}}}}}$      [(5)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- ----------------------------------------------------

where the integral is an MD integral over the $N$-dimensional complex space, and ([[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E5 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) follows from the law of total probability. The MI for an arbitrary MD memoryless channel can then be expressed as

  -- ----- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $I$   ${\phantom{} = {m + {\frac{1}{M}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{M}{\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.\hspace{0pt}g_{i}^{I}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}}}}}}},$      [(6)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

with

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     ${{g_{i}^{I}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}} = {\log_{2}\frac{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.}{\sum_{j = 1}^{M}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{j}) \right.}}}}.$      [(7)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

The MI is an AIR for NB-CM. It is also an AIR for MLC-MSD, which can be shown by the chain rule of mutual information

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $I{({\mathbf{X}};{\mathbf{Y}})} = \sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}I{(B_{k};{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{1},\ldots,B_{k - 1})}$      [(8)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

The code rates of the polar coded MLC-MSD in Sec. [[II-A]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS1 "II-A Three Applications of AIRs ‣ II General Aspects of AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref} were designed to match the $m$ bit-wise conditional MIs in ([[8]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E8 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). This is also shown in the second row of Table [[II]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T2 "TABLE II ‣ III-A Coded Modulation ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}.
:::
:::

::: {#S3.SS4 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[III-D]{#S3.SS4.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Generalized Mutual Information]{#S3.SS4.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-d-generalized-mutual-information .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS4.p1 .ltx_para}
Bit-wise (BW decoders considered in this paper (BICM and MLC-PDL) split the decoding process. First, L-values are calculated, and then, one or multiple binary SD decoder are used. More precisely, the BW decoder rule is

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     ${\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}}}^{\text{bw}} \triangleq \operatorname{argmax}_{\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}} \in \mathcal{C}}\sum\limits_{l = 1}^{L}\log\prod\limits_{k = 1}^{m}f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{k}}{({\mathbf{y}}_{l}|c_{k,l})}.$      [(9)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

The BW decoding rule in ([[9]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E9 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is not the same as the ML rule in ([[2]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E2 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and the MI is in general not an achievable rate with a BW decoder.[^12^[[^12^ [12]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} An exception is Gray-mapped QPSK with noise added in each quadrature independently. In this case, the detection can be decomposed into the detection of two BPSK constellations, and thus, SW and BW decoders are identical.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote12 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}
:::

::: {#S3.SS4.p2 .ltx_para}
The BW decoder can be cast into the framework of a mismatched decoder[^13^[[^13^ [13]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Mismatched decoding theory\[[49](#bib.bib49){.ltx_ref}, [50](#bib.bib50){.ltx_ref}, [51](#bib.bib51){.ltx_ref}\] can be used to study AIRs when the detector is based on a suboptimal decoding rule. This technique has been used for example in \[[52](#bib.bib52){.ltx_ref}, [53](#bib.bib53){.ltx_ref}, [20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\] in the context of optical communications.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote13 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} by considering a symbol-wise metric\[[54](#bib.bib54){.ltx_ref}\]

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${{q{({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{y}})}} \triangleq {\prod\limits_{k = 1}^{m}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{k}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{k}) \right.}}}.$      [(10)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

Using this mismatched decoding formulation, the BW rule in ([[9]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E9 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be expressed as

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}}}^{\text{bw}} = \operatorname{argmax}_{\underset{¯}{\mathbf{c}} \in \mathcal{C}}\sum\limits_{l = 1}^{L}\log q{({\mathbf{b}}_{l},{\mathbf{y}}_{l})}$      [(11)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where with a slight abuse of notation we use ${\mathbf{b}}_{l} = {\lbrack{c_{1,l},\ldots,c_{m,l}}\rbrack}^{T}$. Similarly, the ML decoder in ([[2]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E2 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be seen as a mismatched decoder with a metric ${q{({\mathbf{b}}_{l},{\mathbf{y}}_{l})}} = {f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}}_{l} \middle| {\mathbf{b}}_{l}) \right.} = {f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}}_{l} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{l}) \right.}$ which is "matched" to the channel. Using this interpretation, the BW decoder uses metrics matched to the bits $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{k}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{k}) \right.$, but not matched to the actual (symbol-wise) channel.
:::

::: {#S3.SS4.p3 .ltx_para}
An achievable rate for a BW decoder is the GMI, defined as \[[55](#bib.bib55){.ltx_ref}, Eqs. (59)--(60)\] \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, (4.34)--(4.35)\]

  -- ----- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   ${\phantom{} \triangleq {{\max\limits_{s \geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\frac{q\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}})}^{s}}{\sum_{{\mathbf{b}} \in {\{{0,1}\}}^{m}}{P_{\mathbf{B}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}})}\hspace{0pt}q\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{Y}})}^{s}}}} \right\rbrack}}.$      [(12)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

The GMI expression in ([[12]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E12 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is general in the sense that it holds for any metric $q{({\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}})}$ and for any symbol distribution $P_{\mathbf{B}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}})}$. To simplify this expression, we make two assumptions. First, we assume that the bits $B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}$ are independent, and second, that the receiver uses the BW metric in ([[10]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E10 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). The first assumption is valid for any encoder that induces a uniform symbol distribution (which is the case we consider in this paper), and is valid for any constellation and labeling. The second assumption is valid for BICM and MLC-PDL where LLRs are computed for each bit independently (more details about this are given in Sec. [[III-F]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.SS6 "III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}).
:::

::: {#S3.SS4.p4 .ltx_para}
Under these two assumptions, ([[12]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E12 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be expressed as \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Theorem 4.11\] and\[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Corollary 4.12\]

  -- ----- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   $\phantom{} = {\max{{{{}_{s \geq 0}^{}{\sum{k = 1}m}}{\mathbb{E}}_{B_{k},{\mathbf{Y}}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\frac{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{k}}\hspace{0pt}\left. (Y \middle| B_{k}) \right.^{s}}{\frac{1}{2}\hspace{0pt}{\sum_{b \in {\{{0,1}\}}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{k}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{Y}} \middle| b) \right.^{s}}}}} \right\rbrack}}$      [(13)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
           ${\phantom{} = {\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}{I{(B_{k},{\mathbf{Y}})}}}},$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              [(14)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where the optimization over $s$ in this case gives $s = 1$.
:::

::: {#S3.SS4.p5 .ltx_para}
The GMI in ([[14]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E14 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be written as

  -- ----- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   $\phantom{} = {\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}{{\mathbb{E}}_{B_{k},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\frac{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{k}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{Y}} \middle| B) \right.}{f_{\mathbf{Y}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}}} \right\rbrack}}$                                                                                              [(15)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
           $= \frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}\sum\limits_{b \in {\{{0,1}\}}}\sum\limits_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}{({\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{i})} \cdot$                                                                                                                                                
           ${{\log_{2}\frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{j}) \right.}}{\frac{1}{2}\hspace{0pt}{\sum_{p = 1}^{M}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{p}) \right.}}}}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}},$      [(16)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $\mathcal{I}_{k}^{b} \subset {\{{1,2,\ldots,M}\}}$ with ${|\mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}|} = {M/2}$ is the set of indices of constellation points whose binary label is $b$ at bit position $k$, and ([[16]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.Ex1 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) follows by using the law of total probability.
:::

::: {#S3.SS4.p6 .ltx_para}
In analogy to ([[6]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E6 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and ([[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E7 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the GMI for an arbitrary MD memoryless channel can then be expressed as

  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   ${\phantom{} = {m + {\frac{1}{M}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}{\sum\limits_{b \in {\{{0,1}\}}}{\sum\limits_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}{\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.\hspace{0pt}g_{i}^{G}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}}}}}}}}},$      [(17)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

with

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${{g_{i}^{G}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}} = {\log_{2}\frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{j}) \right.}}{\sum_{p = 1}^{M}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{p}) \right.}}}},$      [(18)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

The importance of the GMI in ([[17]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E17 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref})--([[18]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E18 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is that it represents an AIR for *bit-wise receivers* such as BICM and MLC-PDL. In both cases, the decoding is suboptimal because the dependency between the bits in a symbol is ignored (see ([[10]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E10 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref})). In view of ([[8]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E8 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the GMI in ([[14]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E14 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be interpreted as a sum of unconditional bit-wise MIs. These MIs can be used to select the code rates in a MLC-PDL scheme, as shown in the third row of Table [[II]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T2 "TABLE II ‣ III-A Coded Modulation ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}.
:::

### [[III-E]{#S3.SS5.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[MI and GMI for AWGN Channel]{#S3.SS5.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-e-mi-and-gmi-for-awgn-channel .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS5.p1 .ltx_para}
We now consider the multidimensional memoryless AWGN channel ${\mathbf{Y}} = {{\mathbf{X}} + {\mathbf{Z}}}$, where ${\mathbf{Z}} = {\lbrack{Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{N}}\rbrack}$ is a random vector whose entries are independent, complex, zero-mean, circularly symmetric Gaussian random variables. The total variance of the noise is therefore $\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2} = {{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{Z}}\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack{\|{\mathbf{Z}}\|}^{2}\rbrack}}$, where the square norm is defined as ${\|{\mathbf{Z}}\|}^{2} = {{|Z_{1}|}^{2} + {|Z_{2}|}^{2} + \ldots + {|Z_{N}|}^{2}}$. The value of $\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}$ is the total variance, and thus, the channel law is given by

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.} = {\frac{1}{{({{\pi\hspace{0pt}\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}}/N})}^{N}}\hspace{0pt}{\exp\left( {- \frac{{\|{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}}\|}^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}},$      [(19)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N$ corresponds to the noise variance per complex dimension. The total transmitted power is given by $\sigma_{\mathbf{x}}^{2} \triangleq {{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{X}}\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack{\|{\mathbf{X}}\|}^{2}\rbrack}}$, and thus, the SNR is defined as $\text{SNR} \triangleq {\sigma_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}/\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}}$. The channel capacity (in bit/sym) of the MD AWGN channel under an average power constraint is given by

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${C = {N\hspace{0pt}{\log_{2}\left( {1 + \text{SNR}} \right)}}}.$      [(20)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

::: {#S3.SS6 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[III-F]{#S3.SS6.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[LLR-based GMI]{#S3.SS6.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-f-llr-based-gmi .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS6.p1 .ltx_para}
The GMI was defined in Sec. [[III-D]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.SS4 "III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref} in terms of the channel observations $\mathbf{Y}$. Most SD-FEC decoder used in BW receivers operate based on "soft bits", also known as L-values or simply log-likelihood ratios (LLRs). These soft bits are real numbers that represent the probability of the code bits. More precisely, the magnitude of an LLR indicates the reliability: an LLR close to zero means that no reliable information on that bit is available. On the other hand, a very large and positive (negative) LLR means that we are very certain the transmitted code bit was a one (zero). Because LLRs are simply another way of representing information, the GMI can be defined and analyzed in terms of these LLRs, as schematically shown in Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}. We discuss precisely this in this section.
:::

::: {#S3.SS6.p2 .ltx_para}
Assuming equally likely symbols, the LLRs for bit-wise decoding are at any time instant $l$ calculated as

  -- --------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $L_{k}$   ${\phantom{} = {\log\frac{\sum_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{1}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.}}{\sum_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{0}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.}}}},$      [(23)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where ${k = 1},\ldots,m$ and $\mathcal{X}_{k}^{b} \subset \mathcal{X}$ is the set of constellation symbols labeled by a bit $b \in {\{{0,1}\}}$ at bit position $k$. To alleviate the computational complexity of ([[23]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E23 "In III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the well-known max-log approximation is often used \[[60](#bib.bib60){.ltx_ref}\]

  -- --------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $L_{k}$   ${\phantom{} \approx {\log\frac{{\max_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{1}}f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.}{{\max_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{0}}f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.}}}.$      [(24)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

In the case of AWGN channels and square QAM constellations, the max-log approximation results in piecewise linear relationships between the received symbol and the LLRs. This in turn makes its implementation very simple, which is partly why the max-log approximation is very popular in practice.
:::

::: {#S3.SS6.p3 .ltx_para}
For the MD AWGN channel in ([[19]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E19 "In III-E MI and GMI for AWGN Channel ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the exact L-values in ([[23]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E23 "In III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and the max-log L-values in ([[24]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E24 "In III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) are calculated, respectively, as

  -- --------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $L_{k}$   $\phantom{} = {\log\frac{\sum_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{1}}{\exp\left( {- \frac{{\|{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}}\|}^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}{\sum_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{0}}{\exp\left( {- \frac{{\|{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}}\|}^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}}$      [(25)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- --------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $L_{k}$   ${\phantom{} \approx {\frac{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}}{N}\hspace{0pt}\left( {{\min\limits_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{0}}{\|{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}}\|}^{2}} - {\min\limits_{{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathcal{X}_{k}^{1}}{\|{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}}\|}^{2}}} \right)}}.$      [(26)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
:::

::: {#S3.SS6.p4 .ltx_para}
For equally likely symbols, and regardless of the LLR calculation and channel under consideration, the GMI in ([[13]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E13 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be expressed as

  -- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   $\phantom{} = {m -}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                               
           $\min\limits_{s \geq 0}\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}\sum\limits_{b \in {\{{0,1}\}}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{- \infty}^{\infty}f_{L_{k}|B_{k}}\left. (l \middle| b) \right.\log_{2}\left( 1 + e^{s\hspace{0pt}{({- 1})}^{b}\hspace{0pt}l} \right)\text{d}l.$      [(27)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
:::

::: {#Thmremark2 .ltx_theorem .ltx_theorem_remark}
###### [[Remark 2]{#Thmremark2.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_theorem} {#remark-2 .ltx_title .ltx_runin .ltx_title_theorem}

::: {#Thmremark2.p1 .ltx_para}
[For the specific case when the L-values are calculated using ([[23]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E23 "In III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), ${I{(B_{k},Y)}} = {I{(B_{k},L_{k})}}$ \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Theorem 4.21\], and thus, the GMI in ([[14]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E14 "In III-D Generalized Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) becomes]{#Thmremark2.p1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic}

  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   $\phantom{} = {\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}{I{(B_{k},L_{k})}}}$      [(28)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

[i.e., the GMI is a sum of *bit-wise* MIs between code bits and L-values. The equality in ([[28]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E28 "In Remark 2 ‣ III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) does not hold, however, if the L-values were calculated using the max-log approximation ([[24]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E24 "In III-F LLR-based GMI ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), or more generally, if the L-values were calculated using any other approximation. For example, when max-log L-values are considered, it is possible to show that there is a loss in achievable rate. Under certain conditions, this loss can be recovered by correcting the max-log L-values, as shown in \[[61](#bib.bib61){.ltx_ref}, [62](#bib.bib62){.ltx_ref}, [63](#bib.bib63){.ltx_ref}\].]{#Thmremark2.p1.2.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic}
:::
:::

::: {#S3.SS6.p5 .ltx_para}
LLRs mismatched to the channel or computed with some approximation result in a rate loss. Different LLR correction strategies can be used to improve the rate as well as the performance of the FEC decoder. Such a scenario was for example considered in \[[64](#bib.bib64){.ltx_ref}\], where the LLRs for a channel subject to both additive and phase noise were matched only to the additive noise part of the channel. It was shown for this channel that different rates can be obtained depending on the type of correction used, and that the decoding performance can be improved by LLR scaling. For more details on this, we refer the reader to \[[64](#bib.bib64){.ltx_ref}\] and \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Ch. 7\] and references therein.

The numerical computation of AIRs for the nonlinear optical fiber channel is discussed in Sec. [[V]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5 "V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}.

::: {#S4.SS1 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[IV-A]{#S4.SS1.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Monte-Carlo Integration]{#S4.SS1.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iv-a-monte-carlo-integration .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S4.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
Monte-Carlo integration can be used to approximate an integral via a finite sum \[[65](#bib.bib65){.ltx_ref}, Chap. 9\], namely,

  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${{\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}{f_{\mathbf{R}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{r}})}\hspace{0pt}g\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{r}})}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{r}}}} \approx {\frac{1}{D}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{D}{g{({\mathbf{r}}^{(n)})}}}}},$      [(29)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $\mathbf{R}$ is an arbitrary random vector defined over ${\mathbb{C}}^{N}$. ### [[IV-B]{#S4.SS2.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Gauss-Hermite Quadrature]{#S4.SS2.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iv-b-gauss-hermite-quadrature .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S4.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
For any real-valued function ${g{(r)}}:{{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}}$ with bounded $2\hspace{0pt}J$-th derivative, the $J$-point Gauss-Hermite quadrature is given by \[[68](#bib.bib68){.ltx_ref}, Sec. 7.3.4\]

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\int_{\mathbb{C}}{{\exp{({- {|r|}^{2}})}}\hspace{0pt}g\hspace{0pt}{(r)}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}r}} \approx {\sum\limits_{l_{1} = 1}^{J}{\alpha_{l_{1}}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{l_{2} = 1}^{J}{\alpha_{l_{2}}\hspace{0pt}g\hspace{0pt}{({\xi_{l_{1}} + {\jmath\hspace{0pt}\xi_{l_{2}}}})}}}}}$      [(37)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where the quadrature nodes $\xi_{l}$ and the weights $\alpha_{l}$ can be easily found (numerically) for different values of $J$. This value determines the trade-off between the computation speed and the accuracy of the quadrature. The approximation ([[37]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S4.E37 "In IV-B Gauss-Hermite Quadrature ‣ IV Computation Methods for AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is exact when $J\rightarrow\infty$. Table [[III]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S4.T3 "TABLE III ‣ IV-B Gauss-Hermite Quadrature ‣ IV Computation Methods for AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref} shows the quadrature nodes and weights for $J = 10$.

::: {#S5 .section .ltx_section}
## [V ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel]{#S5.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#v-mi-and-gmi-for-the-nonlinear-optical-channel .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S5.p1 .ltx_para}
The fiber-optical channel is a nonlinear channel with memory, and thus, the AIRs we discussed in the previous sections (MI and GMI) are (possibly loose) lower bounds on the rates that can be achieved when memory and fiber nonlinearities are taken into account. After a very brief discussion of the capacity of such a channel, the majority of this section is devoted to lower bounds on capacity (i.e., AIRs) for the memoryless demapper shown in Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}. Ready-to-use expression to compute these AIRs lower bounds are presented.
:::

::: {#S5.SS1 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[V-A]{#S5.SS1.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Channel Capacity]{#S5.SS1.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#v-a-channel-capacity .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S5.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
The nonlinear optical channel (where the propagation of the optical field is governed by the nonlinear Schrödinger equation) is a channel with memory. The channel law can therefore be described by the conditional PDF $f_{\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}}|\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. (\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}} \middle| \underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}}) \right.$, as shown in Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}.
:::

::: {#S5.SS1.p2 .ltx_para}
For channels with memory, and under certain assumptions on information stability \[[70](#bib.bib70){.ltx_ref}, Sec. I\], the channel capacity is

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${C^{\text{mem}} = {\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}{\sup\limits_{p_{\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}}}}{\frac{1}{l}\hspace{0pt}I\hspace{0pt}{(\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}},\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}})}}}}},$      [(46)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}} = {\lbrack{{\mathbf{X}}_{1},{\mathbf{X}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{X}}_{l}}\rbrack}$, $\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}} = {\lbrack{{\mathbf{Y}}_{1},{\mathbf{Y}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{Y}}_{l}}\rbrack}$, $I{(\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}},\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}})}$ is defined as a multidimensional MI analogous to ([[3]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E3 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), and the maximization is over all distributions of the random *vectors* $\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}}$ satisfying a power constraint.
:::

::: {#Thmremark7 .ltx_theorem .ltx_theorem_remark}
###### [[Remark 7]{#Thmremark7.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_bold}]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_theorem} {#remark-7 .ltx_title .ltx_runin .ltx_title_theorem}

::: {#Thmremark7.p1 .ltx_para}
[The capacity expression in ([[46]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E46 "In V-A Channel Capacity ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is based on a discrete-time channel model described by $f_{\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}}|\underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. (\underset{¯}{\mathbf{Y}} \middle| \underset{¯}{\mathbf{X}}) \right.$, and thus, it has units of bit per symbol (or bit per channel use). This capacity expression does consider memory, yet it does not take into account bandwidth (or potential bandwidth expansion at high powers). Therefore, this analysis cannot be straightforwardly used to determine the capacity in bit/s/Hz. More details on this issue can be found in \[[71](#bib.bib71){.ltx_ref}, Sec. V\] \[[72](#bib.bib72){.ltx_ref}\].]{#Thmremark7.p1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic}

::: {#S5.SS1.p3 .ltx_para}
For independent and identically distributed symbols $\mathbf{X}$ and a suboptimal receiver that neglects any residual memory after DSP (i.e., for the demapper in Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the memoryless MI ([[3]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E3 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) becomes the largest achievable rate. Such a setup is shown in Fig. [[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F7 "Fig. 7 ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref} by the information-theoretic channel. Furthermore, the capacity in ([[46]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E46 "In V-A Channel Capacity ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) is lower-bounded by

  -- ----------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${C^{\text{mem}} \geq I \geq G}.$      [(47)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

::: {#S5.SS2 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[V-B]{#S5.SS2.6 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI]{#S5.SS2.7 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#v-b-lower-bounds-on-the-mi-and-gmi .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S5.SS2.p1 .ltx_para}
The MI for the (suboptimal) receiver described above and independent and uniformly distributed symbols is given by ([[4]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E4 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). As shown in ([[47]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E47 "In V-A Channel Capacity ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), this also gives a lower bound on the capacity of the channel with memory in ([[46]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E46 "In V-A Channel Capacity ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). We are therefore interested in computing ([[6]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E6 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) with $g_{i}^{I}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}$ given by ([[7]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E7 "In III-C Mutual Information ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}). For the nonlinear optical channel, however, the channel law $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$ is usually not known analytically. The MI can be lower bounded as \[[73](#bib.bib73){.ltx_ref}, Sec. VI\]

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${I \geq \overset{\sim}{I} = {m + {\frac{1}{M}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{M}{\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}{f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.\hspace{0pt}{\overset{\sim}{g}}_{i}^{I}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}}}}}}},$      [(48)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

where

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${{{\overset{\sim}{g}}_{i}^{I}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}} = {\log_{2}\frac{q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.}{\sum_{j = 1}^{M}{q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{j}) \right.}}}},$      [(49)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$ is any PDF (usually called the auxiliary channel). In particular, if $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$ is the true channel law $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$, ([[48]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E48 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) holds with equality. Clearly, the more "close" $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$ is to $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$, the tighter the bound in ([[48]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E48 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}).
:::

::: {#S5.SS2.p2 .ltx_para}
A common choice for $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$ is a circularly symmetric Gaussian PDF with variance $\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}$. The circularly symmetric Gaussian PDF assumption might be suboptimal, but in the absence of a better (non-Gaussian) model, the circularly symmetric Gaussian noise assumption is reasonable.[^18^[[^18^ [18]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} It is experimentally demonstrated in \[[53](#bib.bib53){.ltx_ref}\] that for long-haul dispersion-unmanaged optical systems a Gaussian assumption for the noise is a very good approximation.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote18 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} For this auxiliary channel, and in analogy to ([[57]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#A1.Ex16 "In Appendix A Proof of Theorem ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the lower bound ([[48]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.E48 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) becomes

  -- --------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\overset{\sim}{I}$   $= m - \frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{M}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}{({\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{i})} \cdot$                                                                                                                                                                          
                           $\log{{{}_{}^{}{\sum{j = 1}M}}{{\exp\left( {- \frac{{\|{\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}j}\|}^{2} + {2\hspace{0pt}{\Re{\{\left\langle {{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}_{i}},{\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}j}} \right\rangle\}}}}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}}}$      [(50)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where we again dropped the complex conjugate in the term ${\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}_{i}$ due to the circular symmetry of the PDF $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}$.
:::

::: {#S5.SS2.p3 .ltx_para}
In analogy to ([[50]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex12 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), a lower bound on the GMI in ([[22]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.Ex3 "In Theorem 2 ‣ Proof: ‣ III-E MI and GMI for AWGN Channel ‣ III Information-Theoretic Elements and AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be expressed as

  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $G$   $\geq \overset{\sim}{G} = m - \frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{m}\sum\limits_{b \in {\{{0,1}\}}}\sum\limits_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}{({\mathbf{y}}|{\mathbf{x}}_{i})} \cdot$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           
           ${\log_{2}\frac{\sum_{p = 1}^{M}{\exp\left( {- \frac{{\|{\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}p}\|}^{2} + {2\hspace{0pt}{\Re{\{\left\langle {{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}_{i}},{\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}p}} \right\rangle\}}}}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}{\exp\left( {- \frac{{\|{\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}j}\|}^{2} + {2\hspace{0pt}{\Re{\{\left\langle {{{\mathbf{y}} - {\mathbf{x}}_{i}},{\mathbf{d}}_{i\hspace{0pt}j}} \right\rangle\}}}}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbf{y}}$      [(51)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
:::

::: {#S5.SS2.p4 .ltx_para}
In view of ([[29]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S4.E29 "In IV-A Monte-Carlo Integration ‣ IV Computation Methods for AIRs ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}), the MI and GMI lower bounds in ([[50]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex12 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and ([[51]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex13 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) can be approximated via Monte-Carlo integration as

  -- --------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\overset{\sim}{I}$   $\approx m - \frac{1}{M}\sum{}_{i = 1}{}^{M}\frac{1}{D}\sum{}_{n = 1}{}^{D}$                                                                                                                                                                                                    
                           $\log{{{}_{}^{}{\sum{j = 1}M}}{\exp\left( {- \frac{\parallel {\mathbf{d}}{}_{i\hspace{0pt}j} \parallel {}^{2} + 2\Re\{\left\langle {\mathbf{s}}{}_{i}{}^{(n)} - {\mathbf{x}}{}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}{}_{i\hspace{0pt}j} \right\rangle\}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}$      [(52)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

and

  -- --------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\overset{\sim}{G}$   $\approx m - \frac{1}{M}\sum{}_{k = 1}{}^{m}\sum{}_{b \in {\{{0,1}\}}}\sum{}_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}\frac{1}{D}\sum{}_{n = 1}{}^{D}$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     
                           $\log_{2}\frac{\sum_{p = 1}^{M}{\exp\left( {- \frac{\parallel {\mathbf{d}}{}_{i\hspace{0pt}p} \parallel {}^{2} + 2\Re\{\left\langle {\mathbf{s}}{}_{i}{}^{(n)} - {\mathbf{x}}{}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}{}_{i\hspace{0pt}p} \right\rangle\}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}_{k}^{b}}{\exp\left( {- \frac{\parallel {\mathbf{d}}{}_{i\hspace{0pt}j} \parallel {}^{2} + 2\Re\{\left\langle {\mathbf{s}}{}_{i}{}^{(n)} - {\mathbf{x}}{}_{i},{\mathbf{d}}{}_{i\hspace{0pt}j} \right\rangle\}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}/N}} \right)}}$      [(53)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where ${\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{(n)}$ with ${n = 1},2,\ldots,D$ are samples taken from the channel conditioned on that the transmitted symbol was ${\mathbf{X}} = {\mathbf{x}}_{i}$, i.e., samples from the (analytically unknown) PDF $f_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \right.$. Note that the expressions in ([[52]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex14 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and ([[51]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex13 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) are not lower bounds, but only an approximations to the lower bounds. As the number of samples $D$ tends to infinity, however, these approximations tend to the true bounds in ([[50]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex12 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}) and ([[51]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S5.Ex13 "In V-B Lower Bounds on the MI and GMI ‣ V MI and GMI for the Nonlinear Optical Channel ‣ Achievable Information Rates for Fiber Optics: Applications and Computations"){.ltx_ref}).

## [VI ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Conclusions]{#S6.2 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#vi-conclusions .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S6.p1 .ltx_para}
In this paper, we reviewed, achievable information rates for fiber optical communication systems and showed that they are versatile and powerful design metrics for coded systems. Different ready-to-use approximations were presented and discussed. The main focus of this paper was on the multidimensional AWGN channel. This model is very relevant to current optical transceivers used in long-haul dispersion-uncompensated links where a memoryless demapper is used.
:::

::: {#S6.p2 .ltx_para}
The methods described in this paper can be generalized to non-AWGN channels and also to channels with memory. This methodology could for example be used to design multidimensional constellations and coded modulation tailored to the nonlinear optical channel. This is left for further investigation. All the analysis presented here was also based on infinite-block length assumptions and universal codes. Both finite-block length analyses and analysis of code universality are interesting future research avenues.
:::
