::: {#abstract1 .ltx_abstract}
###### Abstract {#abstract .ltx_title .ltx_title_abstract}

Accurate performance benchmarking after forward error correction (FEC) decoding is essential for system design in optical fiber communications. Generalized mutual information (GMI) has been shown to be successful at benchmarking the bit-error rate (BER) after FEC decoding (post-FEC BER) for systems with soft-decision (SD) FEC without probabilistic shaping (PS). However, GMI is not relevant to benchmark post-FEC BER for systems with SD-FEC and PS. For such systems, normalized GMI (NGMI), asymmetric information (ASI), and achievable FEC rate have been proposed instead. They are good at benchmarking post-FEC BER or to give an FEC limit in bit-interleaved coded modulation (BICM) with PS, but their relation has not been clearly explained so far. In this paper, we define generalized L-values under mismatched decoding, which are connected to the GMI and ASI. We then show that NGMI, ASI, and achievable FEC rate are theoretically equal under matched decoding but not under mismatched decoding. We also examine BER before FEC decoding (pre-FEC BER) and ASI over Gaussian and nonlinear fiber-optic channels with approximately matched decoding. ASI always shows better correlation with post-FEC BER than pre-FEC BER for BICM with PS. On the other hand, post-FEC BER can differ at a given ASI when we change the bit mapping, which describes how each bit in a codeword is assigned to a bit tributary.

## [I ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Introduction]{#S1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#i-introduction .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S1.p1 .ltx_para}
In fiber-optic communications, forward error correction (FEC) is widely deployed due to the severe requirements of the residual bit error rate (BER) of the received data, which can be as low as $\phantom{} < 10^{- 15}$. In research and system evaluations, actual encoding and decoding gives the most reliable performance assessment. However, it requires significant efforts to examine very low BERs and the results are specific to the examined FEC code. If one could quantify the performance without actual FEC decoding, it would greatly simplify system development and characterization. Historically, a so-called *FEC limit*, a certain value of the BER before the FEC decoder (hence referred to as pre-FEC BER), has been employed. It benchmarks the post-FEC (after decoder) performance, to quantify the real available data rates, and to measure margins in deployed systems. On the other hand, information-theoretic tools \[[1](#bib.bib1){.ltx_ref}\] have become increasingly common in fiber-optic communications after the emergence of soft-decision (SD) FEC, which is today mainly used in coherent detection systems with digital signal processing (DSP). For example, transmission techniques are often evaluated using achievable information rates (AIRs), which give an indication of the potential data rate assuming ideal FEC performance. We should also consider the gap between the AIR and system throughput (sometimes called "information rate") and how it is constrained by nonideal FEC performance \[[2](#bib.bib2){.ltx_ref}, [3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}, [4](#bib.bib4){.ltx_ref}\]. Thus how information-theoretic metrics like AIR relate to the system throughput in practically relevant cases needs to be understood in detail.
:::

::: {#S1.p2 .ltx_para}
This paper focuses on bit-interleaved coded modulation (BICM) \[[5](#bib.bib5){.ltx_ref}, [6](#bib.bib6){.ltx_ref}, [7](#bib.bib7){.ltx_ref}, [8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}\] with binary SD FEC, because it has been widely deployed due to its low implementation complexity and design simplicity. We combine BICM with *probabalistic shaping* (PS) with reverse concatenation of FEC and PS which shapes the modulation symbol amplitudes. This is known as probabilistic amplitude shaping (PAS), and it can approach the Shannon capacity as shown in \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, [10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}\].[^1^[[^1^ [1]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} The PS module was called "distribution matching (DM)" in the PAS architecture \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\] and generalized to "PS encoding/decoding" for layered PS architecture in \[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}\]. There are many PS coding techniques, e.g., \[[11](#bib.bib11){.ltx_ref}, [12](#bib.bib12){.ltx_ref}, [13](#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref}, [14](#bib.bib14){.ltx_ref}, [15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}, [16](#bib.bib16){.ltx_ref}, [17](#bib.bib17){.ltx_ref}, [18](#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref}\]. They are characterized by the (average) rate $k_{\text{ps}}/n_{\text{ps}}$ and average probability mass function (pmf) ${\hat{P}}_{|X|}$, where the notations will be explained in Sec. [[II-A]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS1 "II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote1 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} Several reports benchmark the post-FEC BER by employing code-independent metrics. For BICM, an AIR is the so-called generalized mutual information (GMI) \[[19](#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}, [8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}\] under a bitwise reception, and its normalized value, ranging from 0 to 1, has been shown to be a good benchmark of post-FEC BER \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, Sec. IV\]. In contrast, pre-FEC BER is not good for very low code rates, i.e., pre-FEC BER vs. post-FEC BER curves will differ significantly between low- and high-order modulation formats \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, Figs. 3(a), 8(a)\]. A similar metric must be redefined for PAS due to the dependence between bit tributaries and the nonuniform symbol constellations. It has been found that a normalized GMI (NGMI), suitably extended to PAS \[[21](#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}, [3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}\], or asymmetric information (ASI), defined from bit-weighted log-likelihood ratios \[[22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}, [23](#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}\], are good benchmark metrics for these systems. These metrics have been used in, e.g., \[[24](#bib.bib24){.ltx_ref}, [25](#bib.bib25){.ltx_ref}\]. Both NGMI and ASI cover also the uniform (non-PS) BICM cases, which makes these metrics more general than the GMI.
:::

::: {#S1.p3 .ltx_para}
Three important aspects of these metrics have not yet been fully covered in the literature \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, [21](#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}, [22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}, [23](#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}, [26](#bib.bib26){.ltx_ref}, [3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}, [13](#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref}, [15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}\]. Firstly, the sometimes subtle relationships between the used rates and metrics should be clarified. For example, the relationship between the (normalized) GMI \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, [21](#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}, [3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}\], ASI \[[22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}, [23](#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}, [13](#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref}, [15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}\], and achievable FEC rate\[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}\] for PAS has not been sufficiently explained and clarified. Recently, NGMI and ASI have been compared for the first time and found to differ \[[27](#bib.bib27){.ltx_ref}\]. Secondly, the lack of studies of the post-FEC performance benchmarking after nonlinear fiber-optic transmission for PAS. And thirdly, there are yet no investigations of the dependence of post-FEC BER on bit mapping. When several bits are mapped onto a modulated symbol, each bit tributary can have different pre-FEC performances. For example, the least significant bit has the worst performance in a regular binary reflected Gray-coded pulse amplitude modulated (PAM) signal. Thus, the post-FEC BER may differ depending on the bit mapping (how a bit in the FEC codeword is mapped to a bit tributary), even if the other conditions are the same, as implied in \[[28](#bib.bib28){.ltx_ref}, [9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\]. More generally, the bit mapping dependence comes from practical nonideal FEC codes, so code-independent performance metrics cannot take it into account when the bit mapping is chosen not to change *a priori* probabilities \[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}, Remark 5\]. Note that density evolution or exit chart analysis, which can take a practical code into account, would be useful in some cases though they are limited only to the specific code used.
:::

The novelties of this paper are

-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S1.I1.i1}

    ::: {#S1.I1.i1.p1 .ltx_para}

    :::
-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S1.I1.i2}

    ::: {#S1.I1.i2.p1 .ltx_para}
    a detailed analysis of the relations between performance metrics of NGMI, ASI, and achievable FEC rate, and,
    :::
-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S1.I1.i3}

    ::: {#S1.I1.i3.p1 .ltx_para}

    :::
:::

::: {#S1.p5 .ltx_para}
We stress that the above metrics relates to post-FEC BER. We do however recognize that the most relevant system output error rates are those after PS decoding in PAS systems, and that the PS decoding tends to increase BER, as shown in \[[13](#bib.bib13){.ltx_ref}, [15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}, [18](#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref}\] (e.g., 5 to 200 times \[[15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}, Fig. 4\] and 10 to 50 times \[[18](#bib.bib18){.ltx_ref}, Fig. 12\]). The relationship between BERs before and after PS decoding depends on the specific cross layout of FEC and PS codes \[[15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}, Figs. 3\]. Since the BER after PS decoding can be estimated from the post-FEC BER \[[15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}, Sec. VII\], this paper, in the interest of generality, focuses only on performance metrics before FEC decoding that estimates the post-FEC BER.
:::

### [[II-A]{#S2.SS1.4 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[System model and generalized L-values]{#S2.SS1.5 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-a-system-model-and-generalized-l-values .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

We consider the system model shown in Fig. [[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}. At the transmitter, the source bits ${\mathbf{S}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k_{\text{ps}}}$ are assumed to be uniformly distributed and independent. The source bits are converted to the shaped bits ${\mathbf{D}}^{\prime} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{n_{\text{ps}}}$ by a PS encoder based on PAS[^3^[[^3^ [3]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} In this work we focus on PAS, though reverse concatenation PS is more general, e.g., it can comprise different coding/shaping schemes like multi-level coding. PAS shapes absolute amplitudes only in each dimension and does not shape sign bits\[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote3 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}, where ${\mathbb{B}} = {\{{0,1}\}}$ and $(n_{\text{ps}},k_{\text{ps}})$ are the numbers of PS encoder output and input bits per PS codeword. Each circle on the arrows in Fig. [[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} denotes block length conversion, e.g., ${\mathbb{B}}^{n_{\text{ps}}}$ to ${\mathbb{B}}^{k}$. A systematic FEC encoding is applied to the payload bits ${\mathbf{D}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k}$ for PAS, whereby the encoded bits ${\mathbf{B}}_{\text{c}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{n}$ are generated, where $(n,k)$ are the FEC codeword length and number of information bits per FEC codeword. The bits ${\mathbf{B}}_{\text{c}}$ are then mapped to bit tributaries in ${\mathbf{B}}^{\prime} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{m \cdot {({n/m})}}$, where $m$ denotes the number of bit tributaries conveyed by each two-dimensional symbol, and $n/m$ is assumed to be an integer for simplicity. Next the bits ${\mathbf{B}} = {B_{1}\hspace{0pt}\ldots\hspace{0pt}B_{m}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{m}$ are mapped into a two-dimensional symbol ${\mathbf{X}} \in \mathcal{X} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{2}$, where $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathbb{R}$ denote the set of two-dimensional symbols and real numbers, resp. In an FEC codeword, a shaping set $\mathcal{S}$ is $\phantom{} \in \mathcal{X}^{N}$, where ${|\mathcal{S}|} = 2^{{k_{\text{ps}}\hspace{0pt}k}/n_{\text{ps}}}$ and $N = {n/m}$. After being transmitted over the channel, the received symbol ${\mathbf{Y}} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{2}$ is demapped to bitwise *a posteriori* L-values, also known as log-likelihood ratios ${\mathbf{L}} = {L_{1}\hspace{0pt}\ldots\hspace{0pt}L_{m}} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{m}$. The discrete-time, memoryless channel law $p_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{X}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}}) \right.$ will in the following be called the *true channel,* although the memory in fiber-optic channel is neglected.
:::

::: {#S2.SS1.p2 .ltx_para}
The L-values ${\mathbf{L}}^{\prime} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{m \cdot {({n/m})}}$ are bit-demapped to ${\mathbf{L}}_{\text{c}} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{n}$, which are decoded to $\hat{\mathbf{D}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k}$ to recover the shaped bits. The PS decoding is performed on ${\hat{\mathbf{D}}}^{\prime} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{n_{\text{ps}}}$ when PAS is employed. Finally the source bits are recovered as $\hat{\mathbf{S}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k_{\text{ps}}}$. For uniform signaling ${\mathbf{D}} = {\mathbf{S}}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{S}} = \hat{\mathbf{D}}$.
:::

::: {#S2.SS1.p3 .ltx_para}
Under mismatched decoding in a bitwise receiver, the mismatched L-values are called *generalized* L-values \[[8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, Th. 4.20\]. In this work, we explicitly define the generalized L-values applicable to systems with PS for the first time. This is useful to describe and understand the scaling of L-values, channel assumed in the demapper, decoding metric, GMI in ([[8]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E8 "In II-B Achievable rates ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), and ASI in ([[35]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E35 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). The generalized L-values are calculated per bit tributary $i$ from the received symbol $\mathbf{Y}$. The generalized *a posteriori* L-value ${\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{po}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}$ is defined as

  -- --------------------------------------------------------- -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     ${\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{po}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}$   $\triangleq$   ${L_{i}^{\text{pr}} + {s\hspace{0pt}{\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{ex}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}}}.$      [(1)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------- -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

The scaling parameter $s$ is useful to correct the L-values, as done in \[[8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, Ch. 7\], \[[29](#bib.bib29){.ltx_ref}, [30](#bib.bib30){.ltx_ref}, [31](#bib.bib31){.ltx_ref}\]. Here, $L_{i}^{\text{pr}}$ and ${\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{ex}}$ are *a priori* and generalized *extrinsic* L-values, i.e.,

  -- --------------------------------------------------------- -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     $L_{i}^{\text{pr}}$                                       $\triangleq$   ${\ln\frac{P_{B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}{(0)}}{P_{B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}{(1)}}},$                                                                  [(2)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
     ${\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{ex}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}$   $\triangleq$   ${\ln\frac{q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}}\mid 0)}}{q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}}\mid 1)}}},$      [(3)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------- -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $B_{i}$ and $P_{B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}{(b_{i})}$ denote bits for bit tributary index $i$ and the probability of bits $B_{i}$ being $b_{i} \in {\mathbb{B}}$, and ${\mathbf{b}} = {b_{1}\hspace{0pt}\ldots\hspace{0pt}b_{m}}$. The notations $q_{B_{i},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}{(b_{i},{\mathbf{y}})}$ and $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{i}) \right.$ are the joint and conditional probability density funcitons (pdf:s) assumed in the demapper, resp., of $\mathbf{Y}$ and $B_{i}$. We note here that in general the bitwise "auxiliary channel" $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{i}) \right.$ in ([[3]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E3 "In II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) is different from the auxiliary channel $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{b}}) \right.$, the bitwise true channel $p_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{i}) \right.$, and the true channel $p_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{b}}) \right.$ (see Fig. [[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). Scaling of ${\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{ex}}$ by the scaling parameter $s$ in ([[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E1 "In II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) is equivalent to adjusting the bitwise auxiliary channel to ${q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}^{(\text{s})}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{i}) \right.} \propto {q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b_{i}) \right.^{s}}$. Then, the generalized *a posteriori* L-values in ([[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E1 "In II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) are rewritten with the scaled and assumed *a posteriori* probability $q_{B_{i}|{\mathbf{Y}}}^{(\text{s})}\hspace{0pt}{(b_{i}\mid{\mathbf{y}})}$, i.e.,

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     ${\hat{L}}_{i}^{\text{po}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}$                      $=$   ${\ln\frac{q{}_{B_{i}|{\mathbf{Y}}}{}^{(\text{s})}( \mid {\mathbf{y}})}{q{}_{B_{i}|{\mathbf{Y}}}{}^{(\text{s})}( \mid {\mathbf{y}})}},$                                        [(4)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
     $q_{B_{i}|{\mathbf{Y}}}^{(\text{s})}\hspace{0pt}{(b_{i}\mid{\mathbf{y}})}$   $=$   $\frac{P_{B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}{(b_{i})}\hspace{0pt}q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}^{(\text{s})}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}}\mid b_{i})}}{q_{\mathbf{Y}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}},$      [(5)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where $q_{\mathbf{Y}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}$ denotes the assumed pdf of the received symbol. The L-values ${\hat{L}}_{1}^{\text{po}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}\hspace{0pt}\ldots\hspace{0pt}{\hat{L}}_{m}^{\text{po}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}$ are used in place of ${\mathbf{L}} = {L_{1}\hspace{0pt}\ldots\hspace{0pt}L_{m}}$, which is bit demapped and fed to the FEC decoder.[^4^[[^4^ [4]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} In a deployable demapper and an FEC decoder, the demapper output L-value $L_{i}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}})}$ is quantized to, e.g., three to six bits. Then another scaling before the quantization works to change the quantization step $\Delta\hspace{0pt}l$. This will be discussed in Secs. [[II-D]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS4 "II-D Decoding metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} and [[II-G]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS7 "II-G Equivalence of NGMI, ASI, and achievable FEC rate ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}, and Appendix C.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote4 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}

In a BICM scheme, the bit mapper/demapper in Fig. [[1]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} is usually described as bit interleaver/de-interleaver. In a practical receiver we can choose a different interleave length if we keep $P_{|X|}$ unchanged.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote5 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}

While the mutual information (MI) $I{({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})}$ is the highest AIR for coded modulation, GMI is an AIR for receivers using mismatched decoding \[[19](#bib.bib19){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (20)\]. It is defined for any memoryless channel as

  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------
     [GMI]{#S2.E6.1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_markedasmath}                   $\triangleq$   ${I_{q,s_{\text{o}}}^{\text{gmi}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}})}},$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          [(6)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
     $s_{\text{o}}$                                                    $\triangleq$   ${\operatorname{argmax}\limits_{s \geq 0}{I_{q,s}^{\text{gmi}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}})}}},$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            [(7)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
     $I_{q,s}^{\text{gmi}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}})}$   $\triangleq$   ${{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\frac{q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}}\mid{\mathbf{B}})}^{s}}{\sum_{{\mathbf{b}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{m}}{P_{\mathbf{B}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}})}\hspace{0pt}q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{Y}}\mid{\mathbf{b}})}^{s}}}} \right\rbrack},$      [(8)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- ----------------------------------------------------

where the label $\mathbf{B}$ and the symbol $\mathbf{X}$ are a one-to-one mapping, ${\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}\lbrack \cdot \rbrack$ denotes the expectation under the joint probability $p_{{\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{y}})}$, and $q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{b}}) \right.$ is the auxiliary channel and not the decoding metric ${\mathbb{q}}{({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{y}})}$ in \[[8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (4.35)\]. See details in Secs. [[II-C]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS3 "II-C Auxiliary channels ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} and [[II-D]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS4 "II-D Decoding metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote6 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} The scaling parameter $s$ in ([[8]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E8 "In II-B Achievable rates ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) is discussed in Sec. [[II-A]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS1 "II-A System model and generalized L-values ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}. Under general matched decoding (i.e., ${q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| {\mathbf{b}}) \right.} = {p_{{\mathbf{Y}}|{\mathbf{B}}}\hspace{0pt}\left.   -- --------------------------------------------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

::: {#S2.SS4 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[II-D]{#S2.SS4.4 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Decoding metrics]{#S2.SS4.5 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-d-decoding-metrics .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S2.SS4.p1 .ltx_para}
The arbitrary decoding metric is denoted by ${\mathbb{q}}{( \cdot , \cdot )}$ to distinguish it from the other parameters. As shown in \[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (57)\], an ideal FEC decoder will recover the codeword $\hat{\mathbf{D}}$ so that

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\hat{\mathbf{D}} = {\operatorname{argmax}\limits_{{\mathbf{D}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k}}{{\mathbb{q}}{({\mathbf{B}}_{\text{c}},{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{c}})}}}},$      [(24)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where ${\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{c}}$ denotes received symbols ${\mathbf{Y}}^{n/m}$ from the FEC encoded bits ${\mathbf{B}}_{\text{c}}$ from the information bits $\mathbf{D}$ in an FEC codeword. The decoding in a bitwise receiver is called BMD. In a memolyless bitwise receiver,[^9^[[^9^ [9]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} In this work, the FEC-encoded bits ${\mathbf{B}}_{\text{c}}$ (length $n$) in a codeword are assumed to be converted to $n/m$ symbols ${\mathbf{X}}^{n/m}$ for simplicity. In a general BICM system, ${\mathbf{B}}_{\text{c}}$ can be dispersed to $n$ symbols and ${\mathbf{X}}^{n}$ is constructed by FEC encoded bits from multiple codewords.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote9 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}

  -- -------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\hat{\mathbf{D}}$   $=$   ${\operatorname{argmax}\limits_{{\mathbf{D}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k}}{\prod\limits_{j = 1}^{n/m}{{\mathbb{q}}_{\text{bmd}}^{s_{\text{d}}}\hspace{0pt}{({{\mathbf{b}}{\lbrack j\rbrack}},{{\mathbf{y}}{\lbrack j\rbrack}})}}}},$      [(25)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

where ${\mathbb{q}}_{\text{bmd}}\hspace{0pt}{( \cdot , \cdot )}$ denotes the bitwise decoding metric for given transmitted bits ${\mathbf{b}}{\lbrack j\rbrack}$ and the received symbol ${\mathbf{y}}{\lbrack j\rbrack}$ for the $j$-th symbol in an FEC codeword. The optimization of the scaling parameter $s_{\text{d}}$ in ([[40]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E40 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) works to minimize the uncertainty, and helps achieving a tighter bound. The bitwise decoding metric is given by \[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (79)\]

  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------- ----- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\mathbb{q}}_{\text{bmd}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{y}})}$   $=$   ${\prod\limits_{i = 1}^{m}{q_{B_{i}|{\mathbf{Y}}}\hspace{0pt}{(b_{i}\mid{\mathbf{y}})}}},$      [(26)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------------------------------------------------- ----- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

which coincides the right-hand side of ([[15]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E15 "In II-C Auxiliary channels ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). Alternatively, log-domain BMD is employed in practice[^10^[[^10^ [10]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} The linear and log-domain BMDs (([[26]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E26 "In II-D Decoding metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) and ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E27 "In II-D Decoding metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref})) are related via an order-preserving transformation \[[33](#bib.bib33){.ltx_ref}, Sec. 8.2\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote10 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}, i.e.,

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\hat{\mathbf{D}} = {\operatorname{argmax}\limits_{{\mathbf{D}} \in {\mathbb{B}}^{k}}{\sum\limits_{j = 1}^{n/m}{s_{\text{d}}\hspace{0pt}{\mathbb{q}}_{\text{bmd,log}}\hspace{0pt}{({{\mathbf{b}}{\lbrack j\rbrack}},{{\mathbf{y}}{\lbrack j\rbrack}})}}}}},$      [(27)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
     ${{{\mathbb{q}}_{\text{bmd,log}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{y}})}} = {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}{{({- 1})}^{b_{i}}\hspace{0pt}l_{i}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{y}})}}}},$                                                                                          [(28)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where ${\mathbb{q}}_{\text{bmd,log}}$ denotes log-domain bitwise decoding metric, and $b_{i}\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack j\rbrack}$ and $l_{i}\hspace{0pt}{({{\mathbf{y}}\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack j\rbrack}})}$ are the bit and the decoder input L-value from the $i$-th bit tributary in the $j$-th symbol, resp. In ([[27]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E27 "In II-D Decoding metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), the scaling parameter $s_{\text{d}}$ does not influence $\hat{D}$. On the other hand, $s_{\text{d}}$ influences $\hat{D}$ with quantization or truncation of L-values before FEC decoding, which is usual in practical circuitry.[^11^[[^11^ [11]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} The scaling at the demapper output and that at the FEC decoder input are equivalent. In this work, all such scaling are done at the demapper. See footnote 4, Sec. [[II-G]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS7 "II-G Equivalence of NGMI, ASI, and achievable FEC rate ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}, and Appendix C about the scaling at the output of the demapper.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote11 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}

::: {#S2.SS5 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[II-E]{#S2.SS5.4 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Performance metrics]{#S2.SS5.5 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-e-performance-metrics .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S2.SS5.p1 .ltx_para}
A common performance target in deployable systems is that the post-FEC BER,

  -- ---------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\text{BER}_{\text{post}}$   $\triangleq$   ${\sum\limits_{b \in {\mathbb{B}}}{P_{D,\hat{D}}\hspace{0pt}{(b,{1 - b})}}},$      [(29)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ---------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

must be very small, i.e., $\text{BER}_{\text{post}} < 10^{- 15}$. To evaluate if the obtained performance corresponds to error-free operation, so-called *FEC limits* have been utilized. This concept relies on the assumption that there exists a one-to-one mapping between the post-FEC BER and certain metrics before FEC decoding. If true it would enable accurate performance estimations in experiments or simulations without implementing FEC. The most common metric has been the BER before FEC decoding, pre-FEC BER, defined as

  -- --------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\text{BER}_{\text{pre}}$   $\triangleq$   ${\sum\limits_{b \in {\mathbb{B}}}{P_{B,{\text{sign}\hspace{0pt}{(L)}}}\hspace{0pt}{(b,{1 - b})}}},$      [(30)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $L \in {\mathbb{R}}$ (without the index $i$) denotes "mixed" L-value in an FEC codeword. The variable $L$ therefore denotes an element of the L-value vector before FEC decoding, ${\mathbf{L}}_{\text{c}} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{n}$. Historically, this metric was used for hard-decision (HD) FEC, and it is accurate for memoryless HD systems. However, its accuracy is not enough for SD-FEC, particularly at lower code rates \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}, Fig. 3(a), 8(a)\], \[[34](#bib.bib34){.ltx_ref}, Fig. 3\]. Instead, $\text{GMI}/m$ was introduced and worked well for uniform signaling with SD-FEC.[^12^[[^12^ [12]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} If there are burst errors and the FEC codeword length is limited, interleaving over time is required to disperse the errors. If not, the post-FEC BER will be degraded compared with the expectation. More details are given in \[[35](#bib.bib35){.ltx_ref}, Sec. II-B\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote12 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} Its value range is limited to \[0,1\] for uniform signaling. To extend this to the PAS case, normalization of the BMD rate $R_{\text{bmd}}$ by its maximum value ${\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}$ was introduced as the normalized AIR \[[22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (7)\]

  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\text{Normalized AIR} \triangleq {R_{\text{bmd}}/{{\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}}}}.$      [(31)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

The normalized AIR in ([[31]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E31 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) does not work for PAS, as shown in \[[22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}\].
:::

::: {#S2.SS5.p2 .ltx_para}
Instead of the simple normalization in ([[31]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E31 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), a useful metric is

  -- --------------------------------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     [NGMI]{#S2.E32.1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_markedasmath}   $\triangleq$   ${1 - {{({{{\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}} - \text{GMI}})}/m}},$                                                                                 [(32)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
                                                         $=$            ${1 - {\left( {{{\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}} - {I_{q,s_{\text{o}}}^{\text{gmi}}\hspace{0pt}{({\mathbf{B}},{\mathbf{Y}})}}} \right)/m}}.$      [(33)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------- -------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

The NGMI was first studied in \[[34](#bib.bib34){.ltx_ref}\] for uniform signals and later extended to PS in \[[21](#bib.bib21){.ltx_ref}\], \[[3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (15)\].[^13^[[^13^ [13]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Here ([[10]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E10 "In II-B Achievable rates ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) and ([[11]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E11 "In II-B Achievable rates ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) are applied to ([[32]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E32 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). In the regime $\Delta_{\mathbb{H}} \geq 0$, $\text{NGMI} = {1 - {\left( {{{\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}} - R_{\text{bmd}}} \right)/m}}$.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote13 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} In ([[33]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E33 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), we generalize the concept further by including the optimization over $s$, which is important for bitwise mismatched decoding. The NGMI in ([[33]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E33 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) with $s_{\text{o}} = 1$ can be shown to be the same as the *achievable binary code rate* (ABC rate) defined in \[[26](#bib.bib26){.ltx_ref}, Example 4.1\]

  -- --------------------------------- -------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\text{NGMI} = \text{ABC rate}$   $\triangleq$   ${1 - {\frac{1}{m}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}{{\mathbb{H}}{(B_{i}\mid{\mathbf{Y}})}}}}}.$      [(34)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------- -------------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

In other words, the NGMI in ([[33]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E33 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) coincides with the ABC rate when the bitwise auxiliary channel is matched to the bitwise true channel (${q_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b) \right.} = {p_{{\mathbf{Y}}|B_{i}}\hspace{0pt}\left. ({\mathbf{y}} \middle| b) \right.}$, and thus, $s_{\text{o}} = 1$). The ABC rate is therefore a special case of the NGMI, and thus, from now on we will not discuss the ABC rate any further.
:::

The NGMI gives the maximum FEC code rate for error-free operation with an ideal FEC in the PAS scheme, as explained in \[[22](#bib.bib22){.ltx_ref}, Sec. III\],\[[3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}, Sec. IV\], and also in Sec. [[II-F]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS6 "II-F Information rate and code rate with rate loss ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} in this paper.

### [[II-F]{#S2.SS6.4 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Information rate and code rate with rate loss]{#S2.SS6.5 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#ii-f-information-rate-and-code-rate-with-rate-loss .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S2.SS6.p1 .ltx_para}
The system throughput (without any overhead) is characterized not only by the AIR, but also by the rate loss due to nonideal FEC[^15^[[^15^ [15]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} The difference between NGMI or ASI and the FEC code rate $R_{\text{c}}$ is called coding gap in \[[3](#bib.bib3){.ltx_ref}, Sec. IV\]. It was considered also in \[[4](#bib.bib4){.ltx_ref}\], \[[37](#bib.bib37){.ltx_ref}, Tab. II\].]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote15 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} and PS coding.[^16^[[^16^ [16]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Overheads for framing and pilot signals should be also be accounted for in practice.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote16 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} The information rate $R$, which is the AIR minus FEC and PS coding rate losses, can be written as

  -- ----- -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $R$   $\triangleq$   ${{{\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}} - R_{\text{loss}} - {{({1 - R_{\text{c}}})}\hspace{0pt}m}},$      [(44)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----- -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

where $R_{\text{c}} = {k/n}$ is the FEC code rate, and $R_{\text{loss}}$ denotes the PS coding rate loss \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Sec. V-B\], \[[14](#bib.bib14){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (4)\]. The obtained AIR from the shaping set size and uncertainty in ([[12]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E12 "In II-B Achievable rates ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) includes the rate loss in PS. At a sufficiently large QAM symbol length $N$ in a PS codeword, ${\log_{2}{|\mathcal{S}|}}/N$ approaches ${\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}$ with a negligible rate loss. See details in \[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}, Sec. II-D, Sec. III-C, Th. 1, Sec. VI-D, Sec. VII-G\]. Note that here we define it per two dimensions, as also done in \[[15](#bib.bib15){.ltx_ref}, Eq. (3)\]. Example values of ${\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})}$, and $R_{\text{loss}}$ can be found in Sec. [[III-B]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.SS2 "III-B Simulation results over the Gaussian channel under matched decoding ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}, Tab. [[II]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T2 "TABLE II ‣ III-B Simulation results over the Gaussian channel under matched decoding ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}. ## [III ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases]{#S3.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#iii-benchmarking-in-various-bit-mapping-and-modulationshaping-cases .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S3.p1 .ltx_para}
As explained in \[[10](#bib.bib10){.ltx_ref}, Remark 5\], bit mapping will not change the performance metrics before the FEC decoding. On the other hand, it can influence the performance after practical FEC decoding.
:::

::: {#S3.p2 .ltx_para}
While quaternary phase-shift keying (QPSK) has only a single bit per in-phase and quadrature component, high-order QAM has several. Therefore, an asymmetric L-value combination over bits for the codeword space, i.e., $\lbrack{L_{\text{a,1}},L_{\text{a,2}},\ldots,L_{\text{a},n}}\rbrack$ where $n$ denotes the FEC codeword length, becomes strongly dependent on the bit mapping for high-order QAM. The post-FEC decoding performance depends on the asymmetric L-value combination. The dependence is stronger in the case of a structured FEC code with a low code rate.
:::

::: {#S3.p3 .ltx_para}
The bit mapping over the codeword should be optimized for the used FEC, modulation, and shaping. In case of low-rate FEC for uniform signaling, the bit mapping affects the post-FEC BER even if the other conditions are the same \[[28](#bib.bib28){.ltx_ref}\], \[[34](#bib.bib34){.ltx_ref}, Fig. 5\]. Examples of good bit mappings for PAS were reported in \[[9](#bib.bib9){.ltx_ref}, Tab. V\].
:::

::: {#S3.SS1 .section .ltx_subsection}
### [[III-A]{#S3.SS1.4 .ltx_text} ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_subsection}[Considered bit mappings]{#S3.SS1.5 .ltx_text .ltx_font_italic} {#iii-a-considered-bit-mappings .ltx_title .ltx_title_subsection}

::: {#S3.SS1.p1 .ltx_para}
To estimate the information-theoretic quantities in Sec. [[II]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2 "II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}, Monte-Carlo integration is a useful way to approximate an integration by a finite sum. If $\mathbf{B}$ is represented by a sequence $\beta{\lbrack\ell\rbrack}$ and $\mathbf{L}$ by a sequence $\lambda{\lbrack\ell\rbrack}$ for ${\ell = 1},\ldots,{m\hspace{0pt}n_{\text{s}}}$, where $n_{\text{s}}$ is the number of simulated symbols, then the symmetrized L-value sequence is calculated as ${\lambda_{\text{a}}\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack\ell\rbrack}} = {{({- 1})}^{\beta{\lbrack\ell\rbrack}}\hspace{0pt}\lambda\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack\ell\rbrack}}$ according to ([[36]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E36 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). Under a matched bitwise auxiliary channel (i.e., $s = s_{\text{o}} = s_{\text{d}} = 1$), the ASI ([[35]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E35 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), ([[48]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E48 "In Theorem 1 ‣ II-G Equivalence of NGMI, ASI, and achievable FEC rate ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) can be computed with Monte-Carlo integration as

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\text{ASI} \approx {1 - {\frac{1}{m\hspace{0pt}n_{\text{s}}}\hspace{0pt}{\sum\limits_{\ell = 1}^{m\hspace{0pt}n_{\text{s}}}{\log_{2}\left( {1 + {\exp\left( {- {\lambda_{\text{a}}\hspace{0pt}{\lbrack\ell\rbrack}}} \right)}} \right)}}}}}.$      [(51)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

While a similar expression is found in \[[8](#bib.bib8){.ltx_ref}, Ths. 4.20, 4.21\], \[[20](#bib.bib20){.ltx_ref}\] for uniform signaling, it is valid for PAS, too, because the ASI works for both schemes as explained in Sec. [[II-F]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.SS6 "II-F Information rate and code rate with rate loss ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}.[^17^[[^17^ [17]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} We believe that ([[51]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E51 "In III-A Considered bit mappings ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) is often used as NGMI in the optical fiber communication field because of its simplicity. Alternatively, one can compute the ASI by a discrete histogram of L-values fed into the FEC decoder. Details are provided in Appendix C.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote17 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}
:::

::: {#S3.SS1.p2 .ltx_para}
Tab. [[I]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T1 "TABLE I ‣ III-A Considered bit mappings ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} shows the examined bit mappings. A bit mapping is denoted as ${\mathbb{M}} = {\lbrack{M_{1},M_{2},\ldots,M_{n}}\rbrack}$ for the bit indexes 1 to $n$ over the codeword, where each integer $1,\ldots,\overline{m}$ must occur $n/\overline{m}$ times in $\mathbb{M}$. Note that $\overline{m} = {m/2}$, i.e., the number of bits per PAM symbol for square QAM, and that $\overline{m} = m$ for cross QAM. To generate a square QAM symbol, two PAM symbols are combined. We consider the following three types of mappings.

-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S3.I1.i1}

    ::: {#S3.I1.i1.p1 .ltx_para}
    *Fixed structured bit mapping*: used for short-period structured bit mappings; ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{FS1}}$ and ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{FS2}}$ in Tab. [[I]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T1 "TABLE I ‣ III-A Considered bit mappings ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}.     :::
-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S3.I1.i2}

    ::: {#S3.I1.i2.p1 .ltx_para}
    *Random bit mapping*: random permutation of bit position in a codeword by a random number, whose seed is changed at each codeword; ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{R}}$ in Tab. [[I]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.T1 "TABLE I ‣ III-A Considered bit mappings ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}.     :::
-   [[•]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_item}]{#S3.I1.i3}

This is more practical than random bit mapping.

The first $k$ and the last $n - k$ components of $\mathbb{M}$ denote the bit tributaries of the information bits and parity bits, resp. For square QAM, the bit tributaries of the corresponding one-dimensional symbol are shown, e.g., three bit tributaries for 64-QAM. Bit tributary 1 determines the sign of the one-dimensional symbol, and bit tributaries 2 and 3 determines the absolute amplitude of the one-dimensional symbol. Usually we cannot control the distribution of parity bits, which are uniformly distributed. Thus not to change the pmf of PAS, we assign bit tributary 1 to the last $n/\overline{m}$ components in all considered bit mappings, where $n - k$ must be $\phantom{} \leq {n/\overline{m}}$ for PAS.[^18^[[^18^ [18]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} Not all bits in bit tributary 1 are assigned to parity bits in the case of ${n - k} < {n/\overline{m}}$, but all bits in bit tributary 1 are placed in the last $n/\overline{m}$ components for simplicity.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote18 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}

::: {#S3.SS2.p5 .ltx_para}
As for the post-SD-FEC BER curves with a random bit mapping ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{R}}$, shown in the upper side of Fig. [[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ III-B Simulation results over the Gaussian channel under matched decoding ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}, ASI is clearly a better metric than pre-FEC BER because the convergence of the curves is significantly better. This random bit mapping breaks the memory in the practical codes[^21^[[^21^ [21]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} The memory break for PAS is limited because the last part of the ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{R}}$ is $\lbrack{1,\ldots,1}\rbrack$.]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote21 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote} However, random bit mapping is too complex to be practical with today's DSP and is therefore only used for benchmarking. Instead the *fixed unstructured bit mapping* can be an interesting practical choice. The curves in the lower part of Fig. [[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ III-B Simulation results over the Gaussian channel under matched decoding ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} show almost the same behavior as for random bit mapping. These results were partly expected, but was not fully predictive in a practical FEC decoding before this examination. The curves of the post-SD-FEC BER vs. ASI for uniform signaling with ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{FU}}$ are converged. We examined nine other random seeds for the fixed unstructured bit mapping, and we found no significant difference in the results. While ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{FS1}}$ fails the benchmark at $R_{\text{sdc}} = {1/3}$, it shows smaller required SNR than the others for PAS-64-QAM by 0.1--0.2 dB at $R_{\text{sdc}} = {5/6}$ or 0.3 dB at $R_{\text{sdc}} = {2/3}$ though the small differences are not clearly visible in Figs. [[4]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F4 "Fig. 4 ‣ III-B Simulation results over the Gaussian channel under matched decoding ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref} and [[5]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ III-B Simulation results over the Gaussian channel under matched decoding ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}. For the all considered bit mappings, ASI is a suitable benchmark of the post-FEC BER, especially for PAS.

## [IV ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Benchmark for PAS-QAM over fiber-optic channel]{#S4.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#iv-benchmark-for-pas-qam-over-fiber-optic-channel .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S4.p1 .ltx_para}
In this section, we discuss the benchmark accuracy of the pre-FEC BER in ([[30]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E30 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), normalized AIR in ([[31]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S2.E31 "In II-E Performance metrics ‣ II Theory for relevant rates and metrics ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), and ASI in ([[51]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3.E51 "In III-A Considered bit mappings ‣ III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). The simulation parameters are listed in Tab. [[IV]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S4.T4 "TABLE IV ‣ IV Benchmark for PAS-QAM over fiber-optic channel ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}. The split-step Fourier method with the Manakov equations was used for simulating the signal propagation over fibers. Lumped optical amplification is used to compensate for the loss of the fiber, and the amplified spontaneous emission noise was loaded per span. In the receiver, the residual chromatic dispersion was compensated in the frequency domain. Adaptive equalization and carrier recovery were performed by fully pilot-aided signal processing\[[41](#bib.bib41){.ltx_ref}\]. The number of taps in the adaptive equalizer was 21 at 2 samples per symbol, and the adaptation was done by the constant modulus algorithm based on QPSK pilots. The carrier phase at a data symbol was computed by a linear interpolation of the estimated phases from the neighboring two pilot symbols, which were recovered by moving average over the previous and the next pilot symbols. In the soft demapping, the SNR for the auxiliary channel (approximated by the discrete memoryless Gaussian channel) was estimated from the pilot signals. The used FEC was described in Sec. [[III]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#S3 "III Benchmarking in various bit mapping and modulation/shaping cases ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}. The resolution and effective number of bits in digital-to-analog and analog-to-digital conversions were 8 and 6 bits, resp.[^22^[[^22^ [22]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_note} The minor differences in the simulation parameters compared to our previous work \[[23](#bib.bib23){.ltx_ref}\] are the target pmf (entropies of a wider range are studied here), the quantization of L-values ($2^{5}\rightarrow 2^{11}$), the decoding iterations ($20\rightarrow 50$), symbol length ($\phantom{} \sim 2^{15}\rightarrow\phantom{} \sim 2^{16}$), number of spans ($5\rightarrow 1 \sim 50$), and codewords per waveform ($5\rightarrow 11$).]{.ltx_note_content}]{.ltx_note_outer}]{#footnote22 .ltx_note .ltx_role_footnote}

::: {#S4.p7 .ltx_para}
Here we can suggest an open problem for possible future research. We start with finding a good (nearly optimum) bit mapping ${\mathbb{M}}_{\text{o}}$, which can be different for each combination of modulation, shaping, and FEC code rate. Then, we quantify numerically the relation between the ASI and post-FEC BER. At least the required SNR performance will be nearly optimum for each signal. Here it would be very interesting to study how the benchmarking accuracy of also the post-FEC BER becomes. Judging from the results in this section, the post-FEC benchmarking accuracy might be good as well. A good bit mapping slightly improves the nonideal performance of a practical FEC decoder, which cannot be taken into account in performance metrics that usually benchmark a post-FEC BER with an ideal FEC.

::: {#S5 .section .ltx_section}
## [V ]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_section}[Conclusions]{#S5.1 .ltx_text .ltx_font_smallcaps} {#v-conclusions .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#S5.p1 .ltx_para}
For the first time, we defined the generalized L-values under mismatched decoding and L-value scaling, and summarized relevant performance metrics to benchmark the post-FEC BER. We then showed theoretically the equivalence of NGMI, ASI, and achievable FEC rate $R_{\text{fec}}^{\ast}$ for matched decoding in a bitwise receiver without quantization and scaling of L-values. We then examined, for approximately matched decoding, where $\text{ASI} \approx \text{NGMI} \approx R_{\text{fec}}^{\ast}$, the metrics to benchmark the post-SD-FEC BER under various combinations of modulation, shaping, and code rates. The limitation of the metric is the post-SD-FEC BER dependence on the code structure in a practical non-ideal FEC. In the case of high code rates, e.g., $\phantom{} \geq {2/3}$, there are less issues, and even the pre-FEC BER would work, e.g., for in-service performance monitoring, if the FEC limit difference is taken into account. The post-SD-FEC BER at lower code rates depends on the bit mapping more significantly. Thus, the ASI, NGMI, and $R_{\text{fec}}^{\ast}$, which cannot take the non-ideal FEC performance into account, do not always benchmark the post-SD-FEC BER accurately. However, when we break the bit mapping dependence by a random bit mapping, or even fixed unstructured bit mapping, the benchmarking accuracy improves significantly. The benchmarking by the pre-FEC BER is significantly worse at low code rates even if we employ a random or fixed unstructured bit mapping. The observation for the Gaussian channel is useful for the fiber-optic channel with nonlinearity as well, though the benchmarking errors become larger for limited data sizes or non-Gaussian-channel signal degradations.
:::

  -- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $I_{q,s}^{\text{gmi}}{({\mathbf{B}};{\mathbf{Y}})} = {\mathbb{H}}{({\mathbf{B}})} - \sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}\sum\limits_{b \in {\mathbb{B}}}P_{B_{i}}{(b)} \cdot$

## Appendix C ASI from quantized L-values {#appendix-c-asi-from-quantized-l-values .ltx_title .ltx_title_section}

::: {#Sx4.p1 .ltx_para}
For quantized L-values $l \in {\mathbb{L}}$, ASI is computed by

  -- --------------------------------------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     [ASI]{#Sx4.E72.1.1.1 .ltx_text .ltx_markedasmath}   $=$   ${1 - {{\mathbb{H}}{(L_{\text{a}}\mid{|L_{\text{a}}|})}}} = {{1 + {{\mathbb{H}}{({|L_{\text{a}}|})}}} - {{\mathbb{H}}{(L_{\text{a}})}}}$                                                                   [(72)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
                                                         $=$   $1 - {\sum\limits_{l \in {\mathbb{L}}}{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}\hspace{0pt}{\log_{2}\left( {1 + \frac{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{({- l})}}{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}}} \right)}}}$      [(73)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- --------------------------------------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

based on the pmf $P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}$. Considering ([[64]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#Sx2.E64 "In Appendix A Proof of Theorem 1 ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), for $l \in {{\mathbb{L}} \smallsetminus {\{{\pm l_{\text{max}}}\}}}$,

  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\frac{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{({- l})}}{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}}$   $=$         $\frac{\int_{l^{\prime} = {l - \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}^{l + \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}{p_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l^{\prime})}\hspace{0pt}\text{e}^{- {\frac{s_{\text{o}}}{s}\hspace{0pt}l^{\prime}}}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}l^{\prime}}}{\int_{l^{\prime} = {l - \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}^{l + \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}{q_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l^{\prime})}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}l^{\prime}}}$      [(74)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
                                                                                         $\approx$   $\frac{{{{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l} \cdot p_{L_{\text{a}}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}\hspace{0pt}\text{e}^{- {\frac{s_{\text{o}}}{s}\hspace{0pt}l}}} \cdot \frac{\text{e}^{\frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}} + \text{e}^{- \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}{2}}{{{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l} \cdot p_{L_{\text{a}}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}}$                                                                                                                                           [(75)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
                                                                                         $=$         ${\text{e}^{- {\frac{s_{\text{o}}}{s}\hspace{0pt}l}} \cdot \frac{\text{e}^{\frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}} + \text{e}^{- \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}{2}}.$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             [(76)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ -- -----------------------------------------------------

For $l \in {\{{\pm l_{\text{max}}}\}}$,

  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     $\frac{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{({- l})}}{P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}}$   $=$   $\frac{\int_{l^{\prime} = {l - \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}^{\infty}{p_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l^{\prime})}\hspace{0pt}\text{e}^{- {\frac{s_{\text{o}}}{s}\hspace{0pt}l^{\prime}}}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}l^{\prime}}}{\int_{l^{\prime} = {l - \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}^{\infty}{p_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l^{\prime})}\hspace{0pt}\text{d}\hspace{0pt}l^{\prime}}}.$      [(77)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------

At a reasonable choice of the resolution $n_{\text{L}}$ and the quantization step $\Delta\hspace{0pt}l$, ${P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l_{\text{max}})}} \gg {P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{({- l_{\text{max}}})}} \approx 0$. In such cases, $P_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}{(l)}\hspace{0pt}{({l \in {\{{\pm {(l_{\text{max}})}}\}}})}$ does not influence the ASI in ([[73]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#Sx4.E73 "In Appendix C ASI from quantized L-values ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}). Then, applying ([[76]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#Sx4.E76 "In Appendix C ASI from quantized L-values ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}) to ([[73]{.ltx_text .ltx_ref_tag}](#Sx4.E73 "In Appendix C ASI from quantized L-values ‣ Post-FEC BER Benchmarking for Bit-Interleaved Coded Modulation with Probabilistic Shaping"){.ltx_ref}), the ASI is approximately computed via Monte-Calro integration, i.e.,

  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
     ${\text{ASI} \approx {1 - {{\mathbb{E}}_{L_{\text{a}}}\hspace{0pt}\left\lbrack {\log_{2}\left( {1 + {\text{e}^{- {\frac{s_{\text{o}}}{s}\hspace{0pt}L_{\text{a}}}} \cdot \frac{\text{e}^{\frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}} + \text{e}^{- \frac{\Delta\hspace{0pt}l}{2}}}{2}}} \right)} \right\rbrack}}}.$      [(78)]{.ltx_tag .ltx_tag_equation .ltx_align_right}
  -- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -- -----------------------------------------------------
