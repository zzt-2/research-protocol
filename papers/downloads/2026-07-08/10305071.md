# A New Meta-Learning Framework for Estimating Atmospheric Turbulence and Phase Noise in Optical Satellite Internet of Things Systems 

Xiaolin Zhou , _Senior Member, IEEE_ , Zhilin Lin , _Member, IEEE_ , Ruoshi Gu , Wei Ni , _Senior Member, IEEE_ , and Abbas Jamalipour , _Fellow, IEEE_ 

_**Abstract**_ **—With the advantages of super-high-transmission rate, anti-electromagnetic interference and good confidentiality, optical wireless communication (OWC) systems play an important role in satellite Internet of Things (IoT). In this article, we propose a new meta-learning-based channel estimation scheme (meta-CE) to address the challenges of atmospheric turbulence and phase distortion in satellite OWC links. A neural network-based channel estimator outputs channel parameters, rather than categories, and utilizes meta-learning to enhance its convergence speed and adaptability to new environments. The meta-CE exhibits superior estimation accuracy, fast convergence, and generalization, compared to baseline schemes, and even outperforms the minimum mean square error (MMSE) channel estimation, especially with short pilot symbols, low-signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and severe turbulence. In a 4 × 4 multiple-input– multiple-output (MIMO) scheme with 8-bit pilot symbols and 0-dB SNR, the mean square error of the meta-CE is about 35% lower than that of the MMSE in a strong Gamma-Gamma turbulence channel.** 

_**Index Terms**_ **—Atmospheric turbulence, meta-learning, phase distortion, satellite optical communications.**

This article is motivated by the fact that severe atmospheric turbulence fading and receiver phase noise seriously affect the quality of satellite IoT communications. Moreover, the turbulence fading and phase noise are tightly coupled. The traditional model-driven turbulence channel estimation methods, e.g., MMSE [12], [13], can hardly deal with the coupling and suffer from poor accuracy, especially under lowsignal-to-noise ratios (SNRs). On the other hand, satellite optical communications typically require short pilot signals, especially when fast-traveling, low-orbit satellites are considered. Unfortunately, the performance of existing model-driven algorithms [14], [15] is unsatisfactory under short pilots. 

There is the potential to apply machine learning for datadriven estimation of turbulence fading and phase noise in satellite optical communications, which, however, has never been studied in the existing literature. A new machine learning technique, called meta-learning, can extract general concepts and learn new tasks more efficiently [16], [17]. It has the great potential to adapt to complex channels with atmospheric turbulence and phase noises, and effectively estimate the satellite optical MIMO channels. Using a meta-learner as the optimizer [18], the learning variable update strategy with gradient descent can converge faster than the standard deep learning (DL) approaches, such as Adam and RMSProp. 

Meta-learning has been shown to be effective in communication systems [19], [20], [21]. Zhang et al. [19] developed a meta-learning-based MIMO detector and showed that the algorithm has excellent generalization performance and performs well in new scenarios. Park et al. [20] proposed a meta-learning-based pilot number selection method and verified numerically the advantages of this scheme over traditional machine learning schemes. Liu et al. [21] proposed a meta-learning-based scheme for modeling the physical layer of optical networks and demonstrated its few-shot learning ability and robustness. No existing studies have considered meta-learning for channel estimation in satellite optical communications. 

The key contributions of this article are outlined as follows. 

- 1) We propose a novel meta-learning-based joint turbulence channel fading and phase distortion estimation scheme for optical communications in satellite IoT systems, achieving finer accurate estimation performance and better robustness than conventional optical channel estimation techniques. 

- 2) We design a neural network-based channel estimator that can improve estimation performance under severe channel conditions. The input and output layers of the network can be adjusted under different MIMO systems and the number of pilot symbols. The structure of the hidden layer is affected by turbulence intensity and noise. 

- 3) We develop a meta-learning strategy to adapt quickly to new scenarios and improve the convergence speed of the model. A meta-optimizer minimizes the loss function effectively and guides the parameter update of the neural network channel estimator.

Meta-learning can be viewed as machine learning with meta-strategies [29]. 

In this article, we adopt optimization-based meta-learning for its fast convergence and strong generalization ability [30]. The proposed meta-CE algorithm extracts the corresponding features by learning the samples in the training phase and establishes a mapping between inputs and outputs in nonideal cases. In contrast, conventional techniques, such as MMSE, use the same strategy in the face of different scenarios, SNRs, and numbers of pilot symbols, which leads to meta-CE showing great advantages over MMSE in nonideal situations. 

The design of a meta-learning-based channel estimator is nontrivial. On the one hand, the efficiency of meta-learning heavily relies on the adequate selection of meta-optimizer, and the adaption of DNNs. On the other hand, the tight coupling of turbulence channel fading and phase noise, and the subsequent complex phase and amplitude changes of pilot signals can lead to an unstable gradient of a meta-learning model, slow convergence or even divergence. 

The proposed meta-CE framework consists of two modules, namely, a DNN-based channel estimator and a meta-optimizer. The channel estimator is the base network when training the meta-optimizer. It estimates the corresponding channel information according to the corrupted pilot data at the receiver of the considered system. The meta-optimizer optimizes the parameters of the base network and improves the model’s ability to adapt to new scenarios and convergence speed. 

We propose to use long short-term memory (LSTM) to serve as the meta-optimizer which is utilized to train the DNN for channel estimation to enhance the generalization ability and its adaptability to fast changing channel conditions. Compared to conventional optimizers, e.g., stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and Adam, the meta-optimizer can converge faster and can adapt to new tasks [18], [30]. 

A meta-optimizer is expected to effectively handle gradient information and guide the optimization process, while also possessing the capability and adaptability to tackle complex optimization problems. LSTM is a suitable candidate for the meta-optimizer for the following reasons. 

- 1) LSTM demonstrates proficiency in processing sequential data, effectively handling time-dependent gradient information within channel estimation models. 

- 2) LSTM is able to retain information across multiple time steps, enabling it to judiciously steer the optimization process using past decisions and gradient information.

## V. CONCLUSION 

In this article, we proposed a new meta-CE scheme for optical communications in satellite IoT systems. The meta-CE can outperform traditional MMSE channel estimation algorithms in different atmospheric turbulence intensity scenarios, especially under low SNRs and short pilots. It performs well in new scenarios with untrained turbulence intensities, corroborating its adaptability to the changing channel environment. 

Through extensive simulations, the scheme demonstrates the excellent ability for generalization and robustness in coherent MIMO satellite-ground optical communication environments. In a 4×4 MIMO with 8-bit pilots and 0-dB SNR, the MSE of the meta-CE is about 35% lower than that of the MMSE under strong Gamma-Gamma turbulence. With the increasing pilot length, the accuracy of meta-CE improves. Additionally, the meta-CE shows rapid convergence. In a 2×2 MIMO scenario with 8-bit pilots and 20-dB SNR under strong turbulence, the meta-CE converges within 20 epochs, whereas conventional DL methods may require over 30 epochs to converge.
