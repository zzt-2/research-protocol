- **Publication status / venue / year**: formally published, IEEE WiSEE, 2024.
- **Contribution**: A data-aided DP coherent receiver chain uses known frame/CFO, MIMO-CE and CPE pilots to retain DSP lock at low SNR. Simulation plus a 3.15 km field trial locates a DSP-outage boundary and states that an aperture below roughly `-1 dB` can worsen combining and should be discarded (`content.md:19-25,100-138,203-211`).
- **Method overview**: resampling → frame sync/coarse-fine CFO → data-aided CE/ZF MIMO → pilot CPE plus BPS → detection (`content.md:33-98`).
- **Experiment setup**: 32 GBd DP-4/16QAM; 100 Monte Carlo trials per simulated condition; 3150 m field link with about 2600 DP-4QAM and 3300 DP-16QAM records (`content.md:100-203`).
- **Baselines**: AWGN theory/pure-AWGN and impairment combinations; blind CMA/BPS are discussed but not given as a task-matched quantitative algorithm comparison.
- **Conclusions**: reliable simulation at `SNR >= 0 dB`; field outage thresholds about `-1.8 dB` (DP-4QAM) and `-1.2 dB` (DP-16QAM), with an explicit warning that statistics are incomplete and operational margin is required (`content.md:138,203-207`).
- **Relation to current topic**: direct defect evidence and mandatory hard-discard comparator; no multi-source validity score, continuous shrinkage or tested combining policy.
- **Implementation details**: 32768-symbol frame, 30720 payload, 2 sps, pilot every 100 payload symbols, about 7.75% overhead; coarse/fine CFO ranges `±500/±62.5 MHz` (`content.md:33-92`).
- **Fit**: high for branch-DSP invalidity and hard abstention; low for soft post-DSP admission.
- **Code**: no open-source code reported.
- **Validation**: internal title/identity PASS; evidence limited to the local qualified fulltext, with no external search in this step.

## Action

Known-sequence correlation and estimation drive deterministic per-branch DSP. A record with `BER >= 0.44` is labelled DSP outage; the paper recommends hard discard of roughly `<-1 dB` apertures before combining (`content.md:106,203-207`).

## Model assumptions
