\subsection{Simulation Setup and Performance Metrics}
\label{sec:simulation-contract}

The evaluation contains an AWGN reference and weak, moderate, and strong downlink Gamma--Gamma turbulence. Fixed-branch curves use 30 independent seeds and 400 processing windows per point on a common 5--35~dB grid. The adaptive receiver is evaluated on the three turbulence regimes over 5--25~dB using the same seed and window scales.

The wider fixed-algorithm sweep reveals the DA and NDA operating regions; the paired adaptive sweep evaluates window-level control over their transition region. The metric is BER, $P_b$. DA excludes pilot positions, whereas NDA carries data in all 256 symbols, so the adaptive comparison scores fixed NDA and the executed output on the same non-pilot payload. The oracle curves remove the true carrier phase (and, under turbulence, the true irradiance) and serve only as performance references.

\subsection{DA/NDA Operating Regions}
\label{sec:da-nda-behavior}

Figure~\ref{fig:ber-overview} shows that the ordering of the fixed DA and NDA algorithms changes in the mid-SNR region. Stronger turbulence also produces a shallower BER waterfall, creating the window-dependent performance variation addressed by adaptive CPR.

The three marked points in Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover} are the SNRs at which the fixed DA and NDA BER curves are equal. They lie between 14.5 and 16.9~dB and are diagnostic curve intersections, distinct from the fixed 13~dB control level.

At 35~dB, the AWGN and weak-turbulence runs contain no observed errors within the simulated bit budget, whereas the moderate- and strong-turbulence BERs remain near $3\times10^{-5}$ and $2\times10^{-4}$. The high-SNR floor therefore rises with turbulence severity.

\subsection{Adaptive CPR Performance}
\label{sec:switching-gain}

We compare adaptive CPR with fixed NDA at the same data-symbol SNR through a common-payload BER ratio. For non-pilot positions $\mathcal C$, let $N_{\mathrm{err},\mathcal C,x}^{(s)}$ be the error count for paired seed $s$ and output $x\in\{\mathrm{NDA},\mathrm{ADP}\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_{b,\mathcal C,x}^{(s)}&=\frac{N_{\mathrm{err},\mathcal C,x}^{(s)}}{768N_{\mathrm{win}}},\\
G_{\mathcal C}^{(s)}&=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{P_{b,\mathcal C,\mathrm{NDA}}^{(s)}}{P_{b,\mathcal C,\mathrm{ADP}}^{(s)}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:common-payload-ber-reduction}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:ber-reduction} reports the mean of this same-SNR logarithmic BER ratio with 95\% confidence intervals. At 9~dB, the reduction is positive in all three regimes and spans approximately 0.8--1.5~dB, with the largest value under weak turbulence.

Across the three regimes, the reduction peaks in the low-to-intermediate-SNR region and decreases toward zero as fixed-NDA errors approach those of adaptive CPR. The benefit is therefore concentrated where fixed NDA is most exposed to low-power windows, rather than uniform over the full SNR range. All three curves peak at the same tested average SNR, 9~dB, while their heights follow the separation available between the fixed algorithms. The common peak location is consistent with one frozen control boundary; the regime-dependent heights bound the gain by the available branch contrast.
