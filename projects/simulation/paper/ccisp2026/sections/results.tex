\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The performance of the DA and NDA estimators, and of the proposed switching scheme, is evaluated over the Gamma-Gamma block-fading channel of Section~II via Monte Carlo simulation. For each operating point, the simulation runs over $N_{\mathrm{blocks}} = 400$ fading blocks per seed (yielding at least $10^5$ bits) and averages over $30$ independent seeds for the benchmark configuration to capture the randomness of the turbulent channel. The SNR is swept from $5$ to $20$ dB for the AWGN reference and from $5$ to $26$ dB for the turbulent regimes, covering both the low-SNR region where the DA estimator is preferable and the high-SNR region where the NDA estimator dominates. Six regimes are evaluated in total---weak, moderate, and strong downlink turbulence; moderate and strong uplink turbulence; and an AWGN (no-fading) reference---spanning the range from benign to severely faded channels. The AWGN reference serves as an implicit baseline against which the effect of turbulence is isolated. The principal metric is the bit error rate $P_b$ evaluated at the $7\%$ HD-FEC threshold of $3.8 \times 10^{-3}$, at which the net SNR gain of the switching scheme is reported.

\subsection{Impact of Turbulence on DA and NDA Performance}
\label{sec:turbulence-impact}

Figure~\ref{fig:ber-overview} reports the bit error rate $P_b$ versus SNR for the DA and NDA estimators across the six considered regimes (weak, moderate, and strong downlink turbulence; moderate and strong uplink turbulence; and the AWGN reference), with BER curves plotted down to the minimum reliably estimable level for each regime. The AWGN reference, included as an implicit baseline, shows the largest gap between the two estimators in favor of NDA, since the absence of fading removes the DA estimator's low-SNR advantage. As turbulence strengthens, the DA and NDA curves intersect: the two estimators are each preferable in a distinct SNR region, and the crossover SNR moves to lower values as turbulence grows, decreasing from $17.9$ dB (weak) through $16.8$ dB (moderate) to $10.7$ dB (strong). Below $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}$ the DA estimator yields the lower BER; above it the NDA estimator does. Two qualitative trends are visible in the data. First, the gap between the two estimators' curves is largest in the AWGN reference, where the absence of fading leaves the DA estimator's pilot overhead uncompensated, and narrows as turbulence strengthens. Second, the crossover SNR moves to lower values as turbulence grows (from $17.9$ dB through $16.8$ dB to $10.7$ dB), so the SNR window in which the DA estimator is preferable widens with turbulence strength. This crossover structure is precisely what the per-block switching rule exploits.

Two regimes are distinguished. \emph{Low-SNR avoidance:} in the low-SNR region ($\gamma_{\mathrm{blk}} < \gamma_{\mathrm{th}}$), selecting the DA estimator avoids the NDA estimator's squaring-loss-driven degradation, yielding a net SNR gain of $1.3\text{--}2.3$ dB over a fixed NDA estimator. \emph{Strong-turbulence net gain:} in the strong-turbulence and uplink regimes the scheme attains a net SNR gain of about $1.2\text{--}1.9$ dB (naive, net of pilot overhead), specifically about $1.3$ dB (downlink strong), about $1.2$ dB (uplink moderate), and about $1.9$ dB (uplink strong). Across all 29 operating points, the scheme selects the lower-BER estimator in 26 of the 29 points, recovering the gain that either estimator used alone would forgo whenever the two estimators disagree.
