\section{Introduction}

The continued deployment of low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite constellations is increasing the capacity required from feeder links between satellites and optical ground stations. Coherent free-space optical (FSO) communication is attractive for this role because coherent reception offers high sensitivity, supports spectrally efficient higher-order modulation, and enables digital compensation of carrier impairments~\cite{valjus2025dsp,paillier2020spaceground}. The atmospheric path nevertheless introduces absorption, scattering, and refractive-index fluctuations. Turbulence produces scintillation in the received irradiance and perturbs the optical phase, while satellite motion and finite laser linewidth add time-varying carrier-frequency and phase errors~\cite{alhabash2001,paillier2020spaceground}. These effects make carrier phase recovery (CPR) a central receiver function: the residual phase must be estimated accurately enough to preserve the decision regions of a high-order constellation as the received signal quality changes over time. Recent coherent-FSO studies consequently combine atmospheric mitigation with digital carrier synchronization or robust data-aided processing~\cite{paillier2020spaceground,johst2024dataaided}.

Data-aided (DA) and non-data-aided (NDA) carrier estimators provide complementary ways to obtain that phase reference. A DA receiver inserts known pilot symbols, allowing the receiver to form direct phase observations and fit the carrier evolution from regularly spaced references. Data-aided processing has demonstrated stable coherent reception at very low signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) in free-space experiments~\cite{johst2024dataaided}. An NDA receiver instead removes the modulation phase from the received data. The classical Viterbi--Viterbi construction raises phase-shift-keyed samples to the rotational-symmetry order before averaging their phases~\cite{viterbi1983}; the same principle extends to amplitude-phase-shift-keyed constellations by using the number of phase points on each ring~\cite{du2025nda}. NDA therefore retains every transmitted position for payload and can aggregate the full observation window, whereas DA obtains explicit references at a prescribed pilot density. Under block-varying Gamma--Gamma irradiance, the effective SNR seen by successive processing windows changes with the atmospheric gain. The two reference strategies can consequently favor different operating regions, creating a direct motivation for receiver-side selection across these regions.

This paper develops a blockwise DA/NDA selector for a coherent satellite--ground FSO link using \((8,8)\) 16-ary amplitude-phase shift keying (16APSK). The selector applies one fixed two-layer decision procedure to every 256-sample digital-signal-processing window. First, a pre-calibrated coefficient-of-variation boundary screens the raw received-power samples. For the remaining windows, a blind received-power proxy is converted to an effective SNR and compared with a fixed 13~dB decision level. This information flow uses quantities available before either carrier-recovery branch produces decoded bits and operates with a shared configuration across turbulence regimes. The resulting study makes three contributions. First, it states a reproducible signal, block, pilot, and total-energy contract that distinguishes the 100-symbol atmospheric channel block from the 256-sample receiver window. Second, across a prescribed grid of 29 additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) and downlink-turbulence operating points, the selector recovers the fixed estimator with the lower data-bit error rate (BER) at 26 points. Third, Fig.~\ref{fig:ber-reduction} quantifies same-SNR BER-ratio reduction relative to fixed NDA, defined by \(10\log_{10}(P_{b,\mathrm{NDA}}/P_{b,\mathrm{SW}})\). Representative reductions are 2.3~dB at 10~dB SNR in weak turbulence, 2.0~dB at 10~dB in moderate turbulence, and 1.3~dB at 5~dB in strong turbulence. These values directly report the logarithmic BER ratio at common SNRs. Separately, in the predefined high-SNR work region, the NDA estimator shows an equivalent-SNR advantage of up to 1.9~dB over DA after applying the stated pilot-energy coordinate. This second result reports the NDA--DA work-region advantage on the total-energy coordinate.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~II defines the signal impairments, atmospheric and receiver block structure, pilot pattern, and SNR convention. Section~III describes the DA and NDA estimators, the two-layer selection statistics, and the fixed decision parameters. Section~IV presents the simulation and metric contract, the complementary DA/NDA behavior, and the selector evaluation. Section~V concludes the paper.
