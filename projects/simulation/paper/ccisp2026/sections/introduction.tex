\section{Introduction}

High-capacity feeder links connect satellites with optical ground stations~\cite{horst2023tbit}. Coherent free-space optical (FSO) communication is attractive for this role because coherent reception offers high sensitivity, supports spectrally efficient modulation, and enables digital compensation of carrier impairments~\cite{valjus2025dsp,paillier2020spaceground,guiomar2022coherent}. Turbulence produces scintillation and optical-phase fluctuations, while satellite motion and laser linewidth add time-varying carrier-frequency and phase errors~\cite{alhabash2001,paillier2020spaceground}. Carrier phase recovery (CPR) must therefore preserve the decision regions of a high-order constellation as the received signal quality changes~\cite{taylor2009phase}.

CPR algorithms can be divided into data-aided (DA) and non-data-aided (NDA) classes according to the reference information they use. DA algorithms obtain direct phase observations from known pilots, at the cost of pilot-bearing transmitted positions~\cite{gavert2022pilot,zhang2010decisionaided}. NDA algorithms instead remove the modulation symmetry, as in the Viterbi--Viterbi principle and its amplitude-phase shift keying (APSK) extensions, and retain data on every symbol; their higher-order statistics and phase ambiguity make their noise response different from that of DA processing~\cite{viterbi1983,du2025nda}. Joint maximum-likelihood formulations improve the estimation of coupled timing, frequency, and phase parameters but solve a broader and more complex problem~\cite{wang2022jointml,du2021jointml,liu2025tdml}. Pilot-aided adaptation has also been studied in coherent optical receivers~\cite{wang2025pilotmlsd}.

Other coherent-FSO digital signal processing (DSP) targets adjacent receiver impairments. Multi-aperture combining mitigates spatial fading before demodulation~\cite{liu2023multiaperture}, while blind modulus-ring equalization addresses channel distortion without training sequences~\cite{qin2026blindvae}. These techniques improve spatial combining or equalization, rather than adapting the carrier-reference class as received power changes. Spatial combining and equalization therefore remain upstream DSP functions, whereas DA/NDA CPR determines the carrier-phase reference. Existing DA, NDA, and joint-estimation methods optimize a prescribed observation model. In a turbulent downlink, however, the effective signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) varies between processing windows and changes the relative bit error rate (BER) of fixed DA and NDA algorithms. Window-level adaptation of the reference class addresses how carrier recovery should respond to these changing power statistics.

This paper develops an adaptive CPR receiver for a coherent satellite--ground FSO downlink using \((8,8)\) 16-ary amplitude-phase shift keying (16APSK). A fixed two-stage rule first screens the coefficient of variation (CV) of the received power and then applies an effective-SNR decision, so each window executes one DA or NDA carrier-recovery algorithm. We evaluate the same rule under an additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) reference and weak, moderate, and strong downlink turbulence: fixed-algorithm sweeps identify their BER operating regions, and the adaptive experiment compares the executed output with fixed NDA on a common non-pilot payload.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~II defines the link model, Section~III presents the adaptive CPR algorithm, Section~IV reports the simulation results, and Section~V concludes the paper.
