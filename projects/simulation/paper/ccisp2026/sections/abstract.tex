\begin{abstract}
Coherent free-space optical links require carrier phase recovery (CPR) that remains effective as atmospheric turbulence changes the received power. Data-aided (DA) and non-data-aided (NDA) algorithms form two classes of CPR, with different reference-information and pilot-overhead requirements. This paper develops an adaptive CPR receiver that uses a coefficient-of-variation gate and a fixed 13~dB effective-signal-to-noise-ratio decision level to execute one branch for each 256-sample window. Simulations of 16-ary amplitude-phase shift keying over additive white Gaussian noise and three downlink Gamma--Gamma turbulence regimes use 30 independent seeds and 400 windows per operating point. At 9~dB, adaptive CPR improves the common-payload bit-error-rate ratio over fixed NDA by approximately 0.8--1.5~dB across the three turbulence regimes. The same received-power-driven rule is used without turbulence-specific retuning.
\end{abstract}
