\begin{abstract}
Coherent free-space optical links require carrier recovery that remains effective as received power changes with atmospheric turbulence. Data-aided (DA) and non-data-aided (NDA) phase estimators offer complementary operating regions: pilots support phase estimation when the effective signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is low, while blind estimation preserves every transmitted position for data. This paper develops a two-layer selector that first detects strongly varying received-power windows through a coefficient-of-variation gate and then combines a blind irradiance proxy with a fixed 13~dB effective-SNR decision level. The receiver selects between multi-pilot phase--time least squares and an eighth-power NDA estimator. Simulations of 16-ary amplitude-phase shift keying (16APSK) over additive-noise and five Gamma--Gamma turbulence conditions characterize the complementary estimator behavior. On a separate 29-point selector grid comprising eight AWGN points and seven points in each of the weak, moderate, and strong downlink regimes, the selector recovers the lower-data-BER fixed estimator at 26 points. Relative to fixed NDA at the same data-symbol SNR, representative BER-ratio reductions are 2.3, 2.0, and 1.3~dB in weak, moderate, and strong downlink turbulence, respectively. The results demonstrate that fixed, interpretable statistics can convert the complementary DA/NDA operating regions into repeatable receiver selection across atmospheric conditions.
\end{abstract}
