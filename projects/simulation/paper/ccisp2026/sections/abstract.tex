\begin{abstract}
Coherent free-space optical links require carrier recovery that remains effective as received power changes with atmospheric turbulence. Data-aided (DA) and non-data-aided (NDA) phase estimators offer complementary operating regions: pilots support phase estimation when the effective signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is low, while the NDA phase statistic preserves every transmitted position for data. This paper develops a two-stage selector that first detects strongly varying received-power windows through a coefficient-of-variation gate and then combines a received-power-based irradiance proxy with a fixed 13~dB effective-SNR decision level. The selector chooses between multi-pilot phase--time least squares and an eighth-power NDA estimator. Simulations of 16-ary amplitude-phase shift keying (16APSK) over additive-noise and five Gamma--Gamma turbulence conditions characterize the complementary estimator behavior using per-window ambiguity-resolved NDA evaluation. In the predefined high-SNR work region, fixed NDA provides up to a 3.1~dB total-energy-equivalent advantage over fixed DA; this value comprises a 1.9~dB data-symbol-SNR gap and the approximately 1.25~dB pilot-energy term of the DA branch. The results demonstrate that a fixed, interpretable decision rule based on received-power statistics can support estimator selection across changing atmospheric conditions.
\end{abstract}
